## Playing the jungle — the general file

Loaded for every game alongside his champion's file and the counterpart's. This is the part that
is true whoever he locked in.

### Tempo is the resource, and it is measured in CS

A jungler's currency is time on camps. Everything else — ganks, crabs, objectives, counter-jungle
— is spending that currency, and the question is never "was the gank good" but "was the gank
worth what it cost to be there."

- **CS per minute is the honest read on tempo.** In this app every farm figure — CS at 10, at 14,
  at 20, CS per minute — is camps *plus* lane minions, because a jungler soaks waves and those
  count. Calling that number "camps" is a false statement about what was measured.
- **Dead time is the enemy.** Walking across the map with nothing at the end of the walk, hovering
  a lane that does not gank, waiting on a camp that is not up — these are the losses that never
  appear as a mistake and decide the game anyway.
- A camp that is up is tempo sitting on the floor. Almost nothing is worth walking past it.

### The full clear, and when to break it

The default is: **clear all six camps, take the crab, then look at lanes.** The reasons are level
and gold, and the level one is the bigger of the two — the level advantage from a full clear is
what makes every subsequent skirmish winnable.

Break the full clear for: a gank that is close to guaranteed (hard CC already committed in the
lane, or an enemy overextended with no vision), a crab that is up and uncontested, or an objective
his team is already stacked for. Do not break it for: a lane that is pinging, a fight already
happening across the map that he will arrive at after it ends, or a hunch about where the enemy
jungler is.

**Arriving late is worse than not going.** A jungler who leaves his route, walks to a fight, and
gets there after it resolves has paid the full price and bought nothing.

### Crab priority

Scuttle is vision, movement and a level-up in one, and the crab on the side the next objective
spawns on is worth roughly double the other one. But **a contested crab is a coin-flip fight in a
corridor**, and the correct rule is:

- Take the crab he can take **uncontested** — that means he knows where the counterpart is, not
  that he has not seen him.
- Give up the crab he would have to fight for **at even health, at low mana, or with a wall
  behind him**. Two crabs is not worth one death, because a death costs the crab *and* the camps
  *and* the next objective.
- The crab on the objective side before an objective spawns is the one worth taking a small risk
  for. The other one is worth none.

### Tracking the counterpart by his numbers, not by guessing

Three channels on the enemy jungler's row survive contact with the fog, and one does not. The three
that do are worth more than any hunch about where he is.

- **A level gap says who wins the fight** more reliably than health bars do, because levels are
  ability ranks and stats at once. Two levels down is not a fight to take. His level is exact when
  it is fresh — but his whole row FREEZES while he is out of vision, so read the age the payload
  prints beside it and treat a frozen row as a lower bound of unknown age, never as a reading.
- **A completed item on him that he did not have last time is the single loudest signal in the
  game.** It tells you he backed — so where he is right now is near his own base and heading out.
  It tells you what changed about his damage. And it tells you a spike arrived: contesting the
  same neutral objective that was even five minutes ago is now a different proposition.
- **His death and his respawn timer are read straight off the feed and are NOT fog-gated.** An
  enemy who dies out of vision still shows as dead with the clock counting down. That is the
  hardest fact available about him, and "he is dead for another 24 seconds" against "drake in 45
  seconds" is the most decidable pair anywhere in this app.

**His CS is not his CS, and no farm comparison between the two junglers is available here.** The
client's figure for an enemy is a count of what your own team WITNESSED him kill, not what he
killed. Measured against the post-game timeline of a full game, his counterpart's row read 70 where
the truth was 183 — under-reporting by a factor of 2.6 — and the coach reading that column told him
he was up 71 and then up 92 on a game he was level on farm all the way through. Both of the only
two lines a human grader has ever marked FALSE in this app are that number.

Two inferences the shape of that counter invites, which are backwards and must not be drawn:

- a counter that is CLIMBING means he has been in your team's vision, not that he is safely on a
  route somewhere;
- a counter that has STALLED means only that nobody watched him — which is the normal state of a
  jungler — and is no evidence at all that he has stopped farming, or that he is near a lane, or
  that he is waiting in a bush.

Working out where he probably is from the event log, from the picture and from what his lanes are
doing is coaching, and it is the job. Saying it in the voice you would use for a figure the client
handed you is not.

### Objectives are won before they spawn

The objective fight is decided by who was on that side of the map thirty seconds earlier, not by
the smite. Setup is: the camps on that side already taken, the wave states pushing in, and vision
around the pit. A jungler who arrives at the pit as the timer hits zero, from the far side, with
half mana, has not contested anything.

**An objective the enemy took is a fact about the game, not automatically a fault.** Without
knowing his cooldowns, his team's positions and what was said, a coach cannot say he should have
been there. What can be said is what he was doing instead and what it bought.

### When a gank is worth the tempo

A gank is worth it when: hard CC is already available in the lane, the target is pushed past the
midpoint, the jungler is not walking through unwarded territory to get there, and the camps he
skips are not about to expire. That is a real list and it is often not satisfied.

It is not worth it when it is a coin flip, because a failed gank costs the walk *and* the camps
*and* frequently a death, while a successful one buys a summoner spell or a wave.

**A gank that bought a kill and two plates while a drake went uncontested is a trade, not an
error.** Coach what it bought against what it cost.

### Dying in the enemy jungle without vision

This is the classic jungle throw and it is worth naming every single time it happens. The pattern
is always the same: he does not know where the counterpart is, he crosses the river anyway
because a camp is there, and he dies in a corridor with no vision and no exit. It costs the camp
he was taking, the camps he would have taken, the next objective, and it hands the counterpart a
free lead on top.

The rule that actually prevents it is a rule with a trigger: **if he cannot say out loud where
the enemy jungler is, he does not cross the river.** Take his own camps instead and be at full
health for the objective.

Counter-jungling is only correct when **both** are true: he knows where the counterpart is, and
he is not behind on his own farm. If either fails, taking his own raptors is the better play and
it is not the passive one.

### Deaths, generally

Two death patterns are worth naming above all others: deaths in enemy territory before the ten
minute mark, and deaths while his team has nothing to gain from the fight. Both are tempo losses
disguised as fights.
