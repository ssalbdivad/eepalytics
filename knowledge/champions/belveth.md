Bel'Veth is a **scaling skirmisher** built around stacking. She wants repeated fights and
objective takes that feed her, and she gets stronger the longer the game runs and the more she
has eaten. This makes her the opposite of a champion you coach toward safety.

**Her attack-speed stacks are permanent and they survive her death.** Nothing about being
behind, or about dying, takes power back off her — which is exactly why the fix on her is always
"go and take something" and never "win this one back".

**Her early clear is not fast and her early duel is not free.** The first few minutes are the
weakest part of her game; forcing an early invade or a coin-flip fight before she has stacks is
the most common way the game is lost outright.

**Her power comes from the objective loop, not from lanes.** She converts objective control into
permanent power, so pathing that keeps her near contestable objectives compounds in a way that
ganking a losing lane does not.

**She wants long fights in open space** — dashes, resets, and sustained damage. A short fight in
a corridor is her worst case.

## Live coaching

Half-sentences, said out loud after a number the client has already proved. **No digits** —
a figure here would be a claim about the game, and every figure he hears must come from the
client. Anything with a number in it is refused at load time, deliberately.

Several clauses per key is intentional: a fixed clause per kind means the identical half-sentence
every time that kind fires, and sameness is what made him stop listening. Each one has to end on
something he can DO in the next thirty seconds.

race-behind: you get paid for the long game, so take the camps and stop trading tempo for a coin flip
race-behind: your clear speeds up as the stacks build, so finish the early routes
race-behind: you cannot out-duel him yet, so farm the side he is not on and wait for true form

race-ahead: turn it into an epic monster, the coral is the part of the lead you keep
race-ahead: your lead compounds and his does not, so keep the route full and stay out of his way
race-ahead: spend it on the pit rather than on him, that is where your power actually comes from

restraint: before true form the even fight is your worst version, so go and take camps instead
restraint: this shape improves every minute you refuse it, so farm the level back and come again
restraint: a fight entered a level down has no exit, so keep the dashes and walk away

fight-lost: you want open ground where the dashes and the resets pay, not a short fight in a corridor
fight-lost: hold the spin while their damage is on you, not while you are chasing someone
fight-lost: you arrive rather than start, so let someone else commit and go in second

fight-absent: that fight was decided before you could reach it, the epic monster was not
fight-absent: objectives are the power you keep, so go and take one instead of chasing this
fight-absent: be at the pit before it starts, arriving late is the thing your dashes cannot fix

death: your stacks are permanent, so being behind is never a reason to force it back.
death: each dash direction has its own cooldown, so keep one pointed at the way out.
death: before true form you lose the fight you did not choose, so let someone else start it.

build: he spikes and you compound, so take the objective now and the fight later
build: if that makes the duel worse, go and stack somewhere he is not standing
build: his item is a clock on the phase you are weak in, so farm through it rather than test it

farm-behind: your early clear is slow, so the fix is finishing it, not cutting it
farm-behind: an unfinished route costs gold and tempo both, take the last camp
farm-behind: put the next camps between you and the drake pit
