# Lee Sin, jungle — patch 16.17

## Where he sits this patch

Riot did not touch Lee Sin in the 26.17 notes, and did not touch jungle monsters, Smite, or any objective (Baron, Atakhan, Rift Herald, drakes, Voidgrubs). Nothing about his clear or his objective math changed from the previous patch — if a habit worked last patch, it still works, and if it didn't, the patch is not the reason.

He is one of the most-contested junglers in the pool: op.gg has him at 13.71% pick and 17.03% ban across its 16.17 Emerald+ sample, u.gg at 13.6% pick and 16.75% ban across 225,404 games. His win rate sits near even — 49.25% on op.gg, 50.93% on u.gg and lolalytics, against a 51.73% Emerald+ average win rate. Read that as: he is played constantly and wins about as often as a coin flip, so the gap between a good Lee Sin game and a bad one is almost entirely in decisions, not in the champion. Do not let "Lee Sin is a carry jungler" excuse a game spent on low-value plays.

## What the kit wants (roles only)

- **Q (Sonic Wave / Resonating Strike)** is a 1100-range skillshot that must *land* before the second cast exists. The recast dashes to the target and scales with their missing health, so its value goes up the longer a target has been fought, not down. Every one of his gap-closes, tower dives, and escapes is downstream of a landed Q.
- **W (Safeguard / Iron Will)** dashes to an ally at 700 range and shields both of them; the recast turns him into a sustain unit for a few seconds. This is the ability that lets him keep contributing while behind — a Lee Sin who is losing gold still has a targeted dash and a shield for his carry.
- **E (Tempest / Cripple)** is a 450-range shockwave that *reveals* what it hits, and the recast slows what it hit. Its role in a chase is information plus a slow, not damage.
- **R (Dragon's Rage)** kicks a champion backward and damages/knocks up anyone the kicked body passes through. 110/85/60s cooldown, 375 range. It is both his only hard displacement and his only reliable way to remove one enemy from a fight for a few seconds.

Practical shape: **he needs to be in melee range to do anything, and his tool for getting there costs a landed skillshot and 50 energy.** Every fight he takes should be one he chose, from a side the enemy wasn't watching.

## Clears and routes

jungler.gg's pathing data (its page is labeled patch 26.16, one patch behind, so treat the exact percentages as approximate and the *shape* as reliable) shows no dominant route — Lee Sin play is spread thin across many paths, with the top three being:

- **Blue → Gromp → Wolves → Raptors → Red → Krugs → Scuttle** — the most-played route at 9%, 52.9% win rate. A full clear that ends on a scuttle contest.
- **Red → Krugs → Raptors → Wolves → Blue → Gromp → Scuttle** — 6.1% of games, 48.2% win rate. The same full clear mirrored to a red start.
- **Blue → Gromp → Wolves → Raptors → Red → Krugs → Gank Bot** — 5.7%, 50.5% win rate. Identical to the first, but the sixth camp is followed by a bot-side gank instead of the scuttle.

Two things to take from this. First, the highest-played and highest-winrate route is a **full six-camp clear before the first real play** — a Lee Sin who abandons his clear at camp two for a level-2 invade or a coinflip gank is doing something that is neither standard nor better-performing in this data. Second, the top routes differ only in *what happens after the sixth camp*: scuttle contest versus bot gank. That is the actual decision, and it should be made from where the enemy jungler is, not from habit.

If you deviate, deviate on purpose. Lee Sin's early clear is unremarkable; his early strength is that a landed Q at level 3 with an ally is a kill threat. Deviations that trade a camp for a real kill threat are the point. Deviations that trade a camp for nothing are how the coin-flip win rate happens.

## Spikes and what he wants from a fight

- **Level 3** is the first real threshold: the full Q → recast → E kit exists, and the top routes are built to reach a gank or a scuttle with it online.
- **Level 6** is the largest single jump, because R is his only displacement and his only way to isolate a target. Before 6 he is a ganker; at 6 he is a playmaker. The 110-second rank-1 cooldown means an R spent on a solo kick that doesn't secure a kill is roughly two minutes of not having his best ability — plan an R around either removing one enemy from a fight or kicking a target *into* his own team.
- His R ranks up to 85s and then 60s, so late-game he can afford speculative kicks that early-game he cannot.

What he wants from a fight: **to arrive from an angle the enemy is not facing, on a target that is already damaged** (Q's recast scales with missing health), with a teammate close enough to follow up before the enemy team collapses on him. A 5v5 where he walks in from the front is the worst version of his kit — he has no range, no wave-clear-based safety, and his escape (a second Q, or a W to an ally) requires something to be there to escape *to*.

## Where he is vulnerable, and where deaths cost extra

- **He is committed the moment he dashes.** Every entry burns energy and puts him in melee. If his W has no ally or his Q has no second target in reach, an engage becomes a 1vN with no exit.
- **He is an early-game champion holding a near-even win rate**, meaning the games where he doesn't convert early tempo are the ones dragging the average down. Deaths in the first ten minutes are unusually expensive for him specifically: they cost the clear *and* the gank *and* the objective he was pathing toward, and unlike a scaling jungler he has no later phase that automatically returns the tempo.
- **Deaths on the enemy side of the map, mid-clear, are the worst kind.** He counter-jungles well because he can Q out, but a Lee Sin caught in the enemy jungle with Q on cooldown has no disengage at all. Check that the enemy jungler is accounted for before you cross, not after.
- **Deaths after a whiffed R** compound: the kick is his only hard displacement, and dying with it on a 110-second cooldown means the next two minutes of your team's fights happen without it.
- lolalytics' Emerald+ 16.17 data shows Lee Sin games trending toward wins when the team takes first tower (first tower obtained in 69.17% of his wins). His ganks are worth the most when they convert into a tower rather than just a kill — a successful bot gank that ends with everyone walking away is a fraction of the value of one that ends with a plate or a tower.

## Matchups against other junglers

All three stat sites agree on the direction at 16.17, Emerald+:

**Hardest:** Rek'Sai (Lee Sin at 45.66% over 1,879 games on op.gg), Taliyah (45.75%, 1,130 games), Aatrox (45.56%, 935 games). u.gg and lolalytics independently name the same three as his worst. The common thread is junglers who either match his mobility and win the melee trade outright, or who can contest from range without ever letting him close. Against these, the level-3 skirmish you'd normally take is the losing version of the coin flip — path away from them and take the camps they aren't at.

**Favored:** Zed (52.45% over 2,551 games on op.gg), Ambessa (54.17%, 864 games), and Viego, all three named by u.gg and lolalytics as well. These are matchups where forcing early contact is correct — the win rate edge lives in the phase before they've scaled.

Also on the favorable side of op.gg's 16.17 table with usable samples: Warwick (Lee Sin favored, 2,331 games) and Trundle (748 games). On the unfavorable side with smaller samples: Karthus (1,066 games) and Pantheon (1,057 games).

Treat matchups with only a few hundred games as a hint, not a plan.

## The one-line version

Full-clear to six camps, then decide scuttle-or-gank off where the enemy jungler actually is; land Q before you commit anything else; save R for isolating or displacing rather than as a gap-closer; and remember that a death on the wrong side of the map at minute 8 is the single most expensive thing this champion can do.
