# Gwen — how she actually works, and what the jungle does to her

Written for the coach watching **him** play her. Everything here is champion identity: the
mechanics below are read off the kit, not off a tier list. Where a figure moves between patches
it is left out and said so, because a wrong number sounds exactly like a right one.

**The one thing to understand before anything else: two of the four things that make Gwen strong
are switched off against jungle monsters.** She is a champion designed to fight champions, being
asked to farm a jungle. Nearly every Gwen-specific coaching point below falls out of that.

## The kit, in the order it matters

**Passive — A Thousand Cuts.** Her attacks and her damaging abilities deal bonus magic damage
equal to a **percentage of the target's maximum health**, and she **heals** for part of that
damage — but only the part dealt **to champions**. Two hard limits matter more than the ability
does:

- **Against monsters the bonus damage is capped at a trivial flat amount.** Her signature
  percentage-health damage does essentially nothing to a camp. A Gwen clear is her base damage,
  her Q and her attack speed — the passive is not part of it.
- **The healing is champions-only.** She has no passive sustain in the jungle at all. Everything
  that keeps her health bar up while she farms has to come from her pet, her W, or a potion.

So she is a champion whose power curve is written in champion fights and whose farming is
ordinary. That is the tension in playing her here, and it is worth saying out loud: **her kit
rewards her for going to fight, and the jungle punishes a jungler for doing that early.**

**Q — Snip Snip!** A cone of snips. Its *passive* half is the part people forget: her **basic
attacks build Snippy stacks**, up to a small cap, and each stack she is holding adds an extra
snip to the next cast. The **centre** of each snip converts part of the damage to true damage
and applies the passive. So the ability is worth far more after a few autos than it is on
cooldown-cycle, and it is worth far more aimed through the middle of something than clipped at
the edge. Against minions it does reduced damage and **executes them below a low health
threshold**, which is why she is fine at soaking a wave she walks past.

**W — Hallowed Mist.** She drops a zone at her feet for a few seconds; inside it she gains armour
and magic resistance and is **untargetable to enemies outside it**. It recasts to her position,
and moves automatically if she tries to leave. The jungle-specific clause is the important one:
**monsters outside the mist can still aggro her and attack her, and she takes no damage from
them.** That is her only real clear sustain, and it is on a long cooldown for a few seconds of
effect — which makes *when* she drops it the whole skill. A W spent on the small monsters is a W
that is not there for the buff, the crab fight, or the moment the enemy jungler walks in.

Against champions it is one of the strongest defensive buttons in the game *and* one of the
easiest to waste: it protects her from everyone standing outside the circle and from nobody
standing inside it. Somebody who has already dashed onto her is unaffected by it.

**E — Skip 'n Slash.** A short dash, then a few seconds of bonus attack speed, bonus attack range
and bonus on-hit magic damage. **The first basic attack inside that window refunds a large part
of the cooldown.** This is the engine of everything she does: it is her clear speed, it is her
mobility, and it is nearly-free as long as there is something to hit. The corollary is the
coaching point — **her dash is only reliably available while she is attacking things.** A Gwen
who has been walking has a dash on full cooldown; a Gwen mid-camp effectively always has one.

**R — Needlework.** A long-range needle that slows and applies her passive, recastable twice more
inside a short window, throwing more needles each time. It is her range, her poke, her chase and
her finisher — and because each cast applies the percentage-health passive, it is genuinely
dangerous to a healthy target rather than only to a low one. It is not an engage and it does not
make her safe.

I have not written her cooldowns at each rank, the exact stack cap, the healing percentages or
her damage numbers — those move with patches and none of them are worth a wrong number.

## The clear, and why it is ordinary

Her clear is **E into autos into Q**, with the Snippy stacks banked by attacking before she
casts. It is a fine clear and it is not a fast one, and nothing about it accelerates the way
Lillia's does. Two practical consequences:

- **Her health is the constraint, not her speed.** With no passive healing against monsters, the
  thing that ends her route early is her health bar. That makes her W and her pet the resources
  to spend deliberately, and it makes "one more camp at 30% health" a genuinely bad idea rather
  than a slightly greedy one.
- **The route can be interrupted more cheaply than Lillia's.** She loses nothing stacked when she
  stops. So the rule that matters on Lillia — *finish the route you started* — is weaker here,
  and CS per minute on Gwen reads more honestly as pace rather than as interruption.

**No route is written down in this file.** Her jungle pathing frequencies were not sourced from
aggregate match data, and a route invented here would be a guess presented as knowledge.

## What she wins and what she loses

**She loses the very early even duel to most dedicated junglers.** She has no hard CC, nothing
that stops a champion who has already reached her, and no healing off anything but champion
damage. Her defensive button protects her from range, not from a diver already inside it.

**She wins the extended fight against anything that stacks health, and she wins it more the
longer it runs.** Percentage-health damage on the passive, true damage through the centre of Q,
percentage-health on every needle: tanks and bruisers are her best matchups and they get worse
for them as the game goes. This is the opposite of Lillia's identity in one respect that matters
for coaching — **Lillia wants open ground to circle; Gwen wants the fight to last**, and the mist
is how she buys the time.

**Her spike is her attack speed and her first completed item together**, because Q's snip count,
E's refund and the passive's application rate are all paid in attacks. Before that she is a
farmer with a dash.

## Objectives and fights

- **She is a reasonable solo objective-taker** — attack speed, on-hit damage, and a W that stops
  the pit monster hurting her while it is down. She is worse at the contested pit than the solo
  one, because she has no way to disengage from a jungler who arrives and no CC to stop one.
- **Her best objective fight is with her ultimate up and the enemy stacked**, because the needles
  apply the percentage-health passive to a whole group from a long way off.
- **The mist is the objective tool people underuse.** Dropping it over the pit as a contest starts
  makes every enemy standing outside the circle unable to touch her, which is most of a
  five-person scramble at a drake.

## Where he loses games on her

Written as failure modes, because that is what a coach can catch:

- **Clearing past the health her kit cannot give back.** She has no monster healing. The camp
  after the one that took her to a third of her health is where the game gets thrown.
- **Spending W as a clear tool early in a route** and then needing it two minutes later at the
  crab. It is both her sustain and her only defensive button, and it cannot be both at once.
- **Going to a fight because the passive heals her there.** True, and a trap: the heal is the
  reward for surviving the fight, not a reason to take one she was losing.
- **Walking without a dash.** E is only cheap while she is hitting things; crossing the map with
  it on cooldown is the state she gets caught in.
- **Aiming Q on cooldown rather than on stacks**, and clipping the edge of a camp or a champion
  with a cone whose centre is the whole point.
