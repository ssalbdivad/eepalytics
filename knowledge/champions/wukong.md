# Wukong — the counterpart, and how Lillia plays against him

Read when Wukong is the **enemy jungler**.

## What he is

A bruiser with an armour-shredding attack, a dash that hits several targets, an invisibility-and-clone
trick, and a spin that knocks up everyone near him twice. He clears well, he duels well, he ganks
well, and he turns any group of enemies standing close together into a lost fight. There is no phase
of the game in which he is weak, and no phase in which Lillia should be fighting him head-on.

## The kit, as it affects her

**Passive — Stone Skin.** He stacks armour and health regeneration while fighting champions and
monsters. He gets **tougher the longer the fight has been going**, which is a direct answer to a
damage-over-time champion. It also means he leaves camps in better shape than his health bar
suggested mid-clear.

**Q — Crushing Blow.** His next attack gains range, deals bonus damage and **removes a share of the
target's armour** for several seconds. Its cooldown drops every time he or his clone lands an attack
or ability, so in a sustained fight it is up repeatedly. The armour shred is why his damage ramps.

**W — Warrior Trickster.** He dashes, becomes briefly **invisible**, and leaves a **clone** behind
that attacks and even mimics his ultimate at reduced damage. Practically: **the Wukong she is
looking at may not be the Wukong**, and it is his disengage as well as his trick. In a jungle
skirmish this is the button that makes her spend her cooldowns on nothing.

**E — Nimbus Strike.** A dash to an enemy that sends clones at nearby enemies too, with attack speed
afterwards. It is his gap-closer and his clear accelerant, and it means a camp between them does not
protect her — he can dash to the camp and arrive at her.

**R — Cyclone.** He spins, gaining move speed, **knocking up everyone near him**, damaging over the
duration with a share of their maximum health, and **it can be cast a second time**. This is a
multi-person knockup with a long window. It is the single worst crowd control in this directory for
Lillia specifically, because it is area, it is long, and it happens where she wanted to be circling.

I have not written cooldowns, shred percentages, clone damage share, knockup duration or the recast
window.

## Reading his clear

- **Fast and healthy**, with Q on a shrinking cooldown and the passive regenerating him while he
  fights monsters. He does not need to back early and he is not soft after a full clear.
- **Expect his CS to keep pace.** Even CS against Wukong is the normal state, not an achievement.
- **A Wukong behind on CS has been ganking**, and his ganks convert, so check the lanes rather than
  assuming he fell behind.
- **His level 6 is a genuine step change**, more than his items are. Before the ultimate he is a
  strong duelist; after it he is a teamfight-deciding one.

## His ganks, and what makes them fail

E in for the dash, Q for the shred, and after 6 the knockup holds the target for everyone else. It
is a simple, reliable gank with no setup and no skillshot to miss, which is why it works so often.

They fail on distance and on vision alone — and on a target whose escape is not a straight line,
because the E dash is single-target. The clone is not usually part of the gank; it is part of the
escape when the gank goes wrong.

## What his items tell you

Bruiser items: attack damage with health and resistances.

- **His first completed item** is the point at which his duel goes from strong to unanswerable for a
  champion with no burst. She should be reading it as a hard stop on contesting anything in melee
  range.
- **More durability than damage in his feed** means he intends to be the one who starts fights, and
  the knockup is what he starts them with. That is a positioning warning for her carries, not only
  for her.
- Anti-heal, when it appears on his side, is aimed at someone; it is worth knowing it is there but
  it says little about him.

## What his presence means for drakes and grubs

- **He is a strong pit fighter.** Armour shred against an objective, sustain from the passive, and a
  knockup that wins the smite contest by removing the other jungler from the pit for a second.
- His ultimate in a drake pit is close to the best-case version of it: enclosed ground, several
  people, nowhere to walk. Contesting a drake against a Wukong with his ult up is contesting on his
  terms.
- The clone means a read of "he is at the pit" can be wrong in both directions — it can be him
  standing somewhere he is not, or him having already left.

## HOW LILLIA PLAYS THE WUKONG MATCHUP

**Who wins the early duel: Wukong, and the longer it runs the worse it gets.** Stone Skin means his
armour climbs while she works; the Q shred means his damage climbs at the same time. Her plan is the
extended fight and this is the one counterpart whose passive is literally a scaling reward for
extended fights. Do not take it at even health, at any level.

**Who wins the race: roughly even, and that is not good enough for her.** Both clears are fast and
healthy. A dead-even game against Wukong favours him, because at equal farm he wins every fight the
map forces. **So the lead has to come from objectives and from tempo he cannot answer**, not from a
CS gap she is unlikely to open.

**When she may contest a crab or an objective:**

- Before he has his ultimate, in open ground, with Prance stacked and dust already on him.
- When she has watched him use Cyclone. That is the window; it is not a long one.
- When her team is present and she is the one arriving second with the sleep, rather than the one
  standing in the pit.

**When she absolutely may not contest:**

- At even health, alone, at any point after 6. The double knockup is a longer disable than her
  escape is a plan.
- Inside a pit or a corridor, where the ultimate catches everyone and where her movement speed is
  decoration.
- On the evidence of seeing him somewhere — the clone exists precisely to make that evidence wrong
  for a few seconds, and a few seconds is the whole window.

**The trap specific to this matchup:** fighting him at all. Almost everything about Wukong is
designed for the fight she wants — sustained, melee, in a confined space — and he does it better.
The version of this matchup she wins is the one where she is somewhere else taking something,
arrives to fights after they have started, and spends her ultimate on the moment he commits his,
rather than on the moment she meets him.
