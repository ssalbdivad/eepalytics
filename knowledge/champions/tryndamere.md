# Tryndamere — the counterpart, and how Lillia plays against him

Read when Tryndamere is the **enemy jungler**.

## What he is

A duelist with a self-heal, a low-cooldown dash and five seconds of immortality. He has no mana, no
ranged threat and almost no crowd control — everything he does is standing next to someone and
swinging until one of them is dead, and he has a button that guarantees it will not be him.

## The kit, as it affects her

**Passive — Battle Fury.** He gains **Fury** from attacking, critting and killing, and Fury
passively raises his **critical strike chance**. He therefore gets stronger the longer he has been
swinging at anything at all — including camps. A Tryndamere who has just cleared is a Tryndamere at
high Fury, which means high crit, which means the duel he starts thirty seconds after a camp is
worse than the one he starts cold.

**Q — Bloodlust.** Passively he gains attack damage as his health drops; actively he **spends Fury
to heal**, more with more Fury. Two consequences and both matter: **his health bar is a resource,
not a countdown**, and a low Tryndamere hits harder than a healthy one. Burning him down from full
looks like progress and often is not.

**W — Mocking Shout.** He reduces nearby champions' attack damage and **slows anyone facing away
from him**. That is the detail: the slow applies to people running. It is not much crowd control,
but it is exactly the crowd control that beats a champion whose escape is movement speed.

**E — Spinning Slash.** A dash through enemies that generates Fury, **and whose cooldown is reduced
every time he critically strikes** — much more so against champions. So in a fight where he is
critting, the dash comes back repeatedly. This is his gap-closer, his chase, his escape, and his way
over walls.

**R — Undying Rage.** For five seconds **he cannot die**, at any amount of incoming damage, and he
gains Fury on cast. This is the only thing about him that needs to be remembered at every moment of
the game. Health bars do not settle fights against a Tryndamere with his ultimate up; they settle
fights against a Tryndamere who has already used it.

I have not written cooldowns, Fury values, heal amounts, crit conversion rates or the slow numbers.
The five-second immortality duration is stable across his whole history but is still a tuning value.

## Reading his clear

- **Fast enough, and sustained by Q.** He does not need mana and he heals with Fury, so he clears
  continuously and backs only for gold.
- **Expect his CS to be respectable but not exceptional.** He is not a fast clearer in the way
  Viego or Master Yi are, and a Lillia running full routes should be even or slightly ahead.
- **A Tryndamere behind on CS and ahead on kills is the dangerous version** — his lead is in items,
  and items on Tryndamere are crit, and crit is the whole champion.
- **His Fury is invisible to the app and to the coach.** Nothing in the client's block reports it,
  so any read that depends on it is a guess. What is *not* a guess is that he has been hitting
  things, which is what fills it.

## His ganks, and what makes them fail

He walks or spins into the lane and swings. There is no trick to it. Pre-6 it works when the lane is
already low and fails otherwise, because his only crowd control is a slow on someone already
running.

They fail on **distance and on vision**: he has to arrive on foot, he has no ranged engage, and the
E dash is one dash. After 6 they do not fail the same way, because an ultimate that removes death
also removes the tower dive's only deterrent.

## What his items tell you

Critical strike, attack damage, and attack speed — his build is one of the least ambiguous in the
game, and that makes the completions easy to read.

- **The first crit item is a real threshold**, because Fury already gives him crit chance and the
  item stacks on top of it. He goes from "hits hard sometimes" to "hits hard reliably".
- **A completed first item on him while she has none** means an even-health duel is not an even
  duel, and this is a champion where that gap is unusually large.
- He does not build sustain beyond his Q and he does not need it. The absence of defensive items in
  his feed is not a weakness to exploit; the immortality is the defensive item.

## What his presence means for drakes and grubs

This is where Undying Rage does the most damage to her decision-making.

- **A smite contest against a Tryndamere with his ultimate available cannot be won on health.** The
  plan "I will burst him down and take it" has a five-second hole in it.
- The reverse is the actual lever: **if she watched him use it, there is a window of well over a
  minute — longer at early ranks, shorter later — where he is an ordinary melee champion with no
  escape from a sleep.** That window is when
  objectives get taken.
- He has no ranged steal, no monster-damage bonus, and no way to take an objective quickly. He does
  not out-damage the pit; he out-survives the fight around it.

## HOW LILLIA PLAYS THE TRYNDAMERE MATCHUP

**Who wins the early duel: Tryndamere, if she stands still, and nobody, if she does not.** He has no
way to close a gap she keeps opening except a single dash, and every second he spends walking is a
second of Dream Dust ticking. But the moment the fight becomes stationary — a camp, a pit, a
corridor — he heals, he crits, and she has no burst to end it. And after 6 there is no health total
at which finishing the duel is guaranteed.

**Who wins the race: Lillia, narrowly, and it is worth taking.** Both clears are self-sufficient.
Hers accelerates and his does not, and he spends more time on lanes because that is where his
champion converts.

**When she may contest a crab or an objective:**

- When she has seen him use Undying Rage. That is the single clearest green light in this entire
  directory.
- In open ground, with Prance already stacked, where the fight is a kite and not a stand.
- When she is ahead in levels and can put the sleep on him before he is in range — a slept
  Tryndamere without his ultimate is the one version of him that simply dies.

**When she absolutely may not contest:**

- At even health in a pit or a corridor, at any point after he has his ultimate. His floor is
  immortality and hers is a movement-speed buff that a wall makes useless.
- Into his Mocking Shout while running in a straight line away from him. Facing away is exactly
  when the slow applies.
- At low health believing she has time to leave. Between the dash and the slow-on-fleeing, leaving
  is the thing this champion is specifically built to prevent.

**The trap specific to this matchup:** reading his health bar. He heals with Q, he gains damage as
he drops, and for five seconds he does not die at all. **The health bar is not the information — his
ultimate's cooldown is**, and the only way she gets that information is by watching him spend it.
