# Shyvana — the counterpart, and how Lillia plays against her

This file exists to be read when Shyvana is the **enemy jungler**. Everything below is about what
she is doing while Lillia is clearing, and what it means for her.

**Read the kit below rather than a memory of it.** Shyvana's abilities are Scalemail, Emberstrike,
Inferno Aegis, Molten Burst and Dragon's Descent; the names and the mechanics that were true of her
for years are not the ones in the current data files. Anything recalled rather than read is likely
to be about a champion that no longer exists.

## What she is

A farming fighter who converts camps into permanent defensive stats and into dragon uptime. Melee,
no mana — her ultimate is a **resource bar**, not a cooldown — and a kit built around basic attacks
with a long-range slow attached. She is not an early-game bully and she is not a burst threat. She
is the jungler who is quietly getting harder to kill every time she finishes a camp.

## The kit, as it affects her

**Passive — Scalemail.** Takedowns on **large monsters and large minions** give her a stack;
champions and epic monsters give several. Every stack is a small permanent amount of **armour AND
magic resistance**. Two consequences, and the second is the one that matters most in this matchup:

- **Farming makes her tankier, not just richer.** A Shyvana with a big CS lead is not merely ahead
  on gold in the ordinary way; her defensive stats are genuinely higher than her items suggest, and
  a duel that would have been even at equal items is not.
- **Half of it is magic resistance**, which is precisely the stat that blunts Lillia. Her burn is
  magic damage on a percentage of maximum health, and Shyvana's answer to it accumulates from
  doing the thing she was going to do anyway.

**Q — Emberstrike.** Her basic attacks **take time off its cooldown** and deal bonus magic damage
based on the target's **maximum health**, with a floor and a cap against non-champions — so unlike
some percentage-health passives it is worth real damage in the jungle. The active empowers her next
attack and can be cast twice in a row, three times as a dragon, and gives her a little extra attack
range while it is up. The practical reading: **a Shyvana who is attacking something has this
ability almost permanently available**, and her jungle clear is fast for exactly that reason.

**W — Inferno Aegis.** A short shield that is bigger the more enemy champions are near her, plus a
large burst of **movement speed** that is larger still while she is facing a visible enemy
champion, and a recast that consumes the shield to explode. As a dragon, that explosion **heals
her** when it catches a champion.

This is the ability that breaks the ordinary Lillia plan. Her whole escape is movement speed;
Shyvana's W is movement speed that is explicitly *better when she is chasing a champion she can
see*. A gap that looks safe against most junglers does not stay open against this one.

**E — Molten Burst.** A fireball in a line that explodes on the first thing it hits, damaging and
**slowing**, with a component scaling on the target's **maximum health**. As a dragon it is wider,
passes through, and leaves burning ground behind it. It is her only ranged tool, and it is how
almost every fight with her starts — a slow landed from outside melee range.

**R — Dragon's Descent.** Not a cooldown: a **Fury bar** that fills over time and from damage she
deals, more slowly against non-champions. Activating it turns her into a dragon and **flies her
forward** — displacement-immune, up to a long way — breathing a cone that **fears** and nearly
stops anyone it catches. As a dragon she has more health, more attack range and a bigger body, and
every basic ability gains an extra effect; she stays that way until the bar drains.

**The client does not show her Fury, so the coach cannot know whether her ultimate is up.** Her
level and her completed items are the parts of her the live feed reports exactly. Treat "she might
be about to be a dragon" as the permanent state of this matchup rather than something to guess at.

I have not written her cooldowns at each rank, her resistances per stack, her fury generation rate,
her damage numbers, or whether Scalemail stacks survive a death — those move with patches, and the
last of them I could not confirm at all.

## Reading her clear

- **She clears fast and she farms on purpose.** Expect her CS to keep pace or beat a good Lillia
  route, and read her being ahead on CS as her plan working rather than as an accident.
- **If she is ahead in level and items, she is also tankier than the item list looks**, because of
  the passive. That is the one place in this directory where the usual "read his build" advice
  understates the enemy.
- **If she is behind in level from about 5 onward**, she has been near lanes rather than farming —
  which for this champion is the worse version of her, but it still means the next place she
  appears is a lane.
- **A completed first item on her while Lillia has none** is the moment to stop contesting
  anything neutral: her damage profile is sustained, and sustained damage is what an item buys.

## Her ganks, and what makes them fail

She ganks with the E slow, or by flying in with the ultimate and fearing whoever is in the cone.
The first is dodgeable and the second is not, which is why the honest rule is about the ult being
unknowable rather than about dodging.

They fail for the ordinary reasons: **she is seen walking in** — she has no stealth and nothing
that hides an approach in human form — and **she arrives with the slow already spent**. A Shyvana
whose E has missed and whose fury is empty has a walk and an attack, and not much else.

Once she has flown in, **she has no exit**. Her ultimate is an entrance; the shield buys her a few
seconds and nothing takes her back out. A dive that does not kill is usually a dead Shyvana.

## HOW LILLIA PLAYS THE SHYVANA MATCHUP

This is the section that matters.

**Who wins the early duel: Shyvana, and it gets worse rather than better.** She has sustained
damage, a shield, a slow and stats that grow off her own farm. Lillia has a burn that arrives late
and an escape that Shyvana's W is specifically good at following. No even-health 1v1 before
Lillia's first completed item — not at a crab, not to defend a camp.

**Who wins the race: this is the one matchup where "just out-farm her" is not the plan.** Both
clears are fast, and hers converts directly into resistances against Lillia's own damage type. The
plan is instead **tempo into objectives**: be at the timer first, take the pit with the team, and
spend the lead on something that stays taken rather than on a duel that gets harder every minute.

**The mechanic to plan around: she is faster than she looks when she is chasing.** The usual
Lillia rule — start leaving before the fight starts, the speed is the exit — is *more* important
here, not less, because W closes the gap she is relying on and the ult closes it from much further
out. If she is visible and heading her way, the decision to leave had to be taken already.

**When she may contest a crab or a pit:**

- When she knows where Shyvana is and it is not here. The Swirlseed down the entrance is the
  cheapest way to know that, and it costs two seconds.
- When she is meaningfully ahead in level, at good health, **with open ground behind her** — and
  even then, expecting the fight to be long rather than decisive.
- Alongside her team. A contested pit against a dragon is not a fight she wins alone at any point
  in the game.

**When she absolutely may not:**

- At even health before her first item. Ever.
- At low mana. She cannot fight her and she cannot outrun a W that is faster while facing her.
- In a corridor or inside a pit, where the fear and the slow have somewhere to pin her.
- When she cannot say where Shyvana is. "She is probably topside" is not information against a
  champion with a long flight that cannot be displaced out of.

**The trap specific to this matchup:** treating the early game as the safe window and the late game
as the dangerous one. It is true of most junglers and only half true here — Shyvana is beatable
*early*, but beating her early means denying her camps, which is the one thing Lillia cannot do by
fighting her. The realistic version is: take the objectives on time, and stop expecting a duel to
come back into reach later, because it does not.
