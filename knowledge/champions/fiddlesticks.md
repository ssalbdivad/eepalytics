# Fiddlesticks — the counterpart, and how Lillia plays against him

Read when Fiddlesticks is the **enemy jungler**.

## What he is

A slow, squishy, terrifying ambusher who is nearly harmless in the open and nearly unbeatable out
of a brush. He has no dash, no escape and no way to chase anyone who is already running — and one
of the strongest teamfight ultimates in the game, delivered from somewhere nobody was looking.

**The whole champion is the surprise.** Take the surprise away and there is not much left.

## The kit, as it affects her

**Passive — A Harmless Scarecrow.** His trinket is replaced by **effigies**: fake Fiddlesticks
that sit on the map and grant vision. Two things follow. He has more map vision than a normal
jungler's ward budget allows, and **a Fiddlesticks standing still in a brush might not be a
Fiddlesticks at all** — the effigy exists to make "I saw him topside" unreliable information.

**Q — Terrify.** A fear. It costs him a target's entire escape, which against Lillia means her
movement speed, which means everything. The passive matters more than the active: **while he is
unseen, damaging anyone with an ability fears them automatically.** He does not need to aim the
fear if he is the one starting the fight from a brush.

**W — Bountiful Harvest.** A channelled drain that heals him and scales with the target's missing
health. It is his clear engine and his sustain, and it is interruptible — anything that stops the
channel takes most of the fight off him.

**E — Reap.** A ranged line of damage that slows, and **silences anyone caught in the centre**.
A silenced Lillia has no Q, no E, no ult and no Prance — the silence is the single most dangerous
button he has against her outside his ultimate.

**R — Crowstorm.** He channels for a moment, then **teleports** to the target location and unleashes
sustained area damage for several seconds. The channel is visible and audible, but the point is
that it starts somewhere she cannot see: a brush, over a wall, inside the pit. This is the ability
that decides objective fights.

I have not written his cooldowns, drain percentages, fear durations or the exact channel and
Crowstorm durations. Those move with patches.

## Reading his clear

- **His early clear is slow and mana-hungry**, and his early ganks are worse. Before his ultimate
  he is one of the weakest junglers on the map.
- **So a Fiddlesticks even on CS pre-6 is doing fine**, and a Fiddlesticks ahead on CS has simply
  been left alone to farm — which is what he wants, because his whole game is on the other side
  of level 6.
- **A Fiddlesticks behind on CS is not necessarily behind.** He is the champion most likely to have
  spent the early game sitting in a brush waiting for one ultimate that changes a game.
- **He becomes a different champion at 6 and again at his first completed item.** Level, not CS,
  is the number to watch on him.

## His ganks, and what makes them fail

Before 6 he walks at a lane and fears someone, and it fails to almost anything — he cannot close
the gap and he cannot finish. After 6 he does not gank, he **arrives**: Crowstorm out of a brush
or over a wall onto a lane that had no reason to expect him.

They fail for exactly one reason and it is a reason she controls: **vision on the brush and the
wall he would have to be behind.** He has no way to approach unseen except by having been there
first. A warded pit and a warded brush is a Fiddlesticks with no ultimate.

## What his items tell you

He is an ability-power champion in every build, so the class of item is not the tell — the
**quantity** is. His damage in a fight is delivered over several seconds of Crowstorm, so each
completed item makes the same ultimate much more lethal to stand in. A Fiddlesticks two items up
turns a drake fight into a wipe, whatever the health bars said before he landed.

A completed first item on him is the point at which standing in Crowstorm for a smite stops being
survivable. There is no version of his build where he wins a duel he did not start.

## What his presence means for drakes and grubs

This is the section that matters most for him. **Fiddlesticks does not contest objectives, he
ambushes the fight around them.** The pit is enclosed, and enclosed is the geometry his ultimate
was designed for.

- An unwarded pit against a Fiddlesticks with his ultimate available is not an objective, it is a
  trap with a timer.
- Contesting a drake he can Crowstorm into is worse than losing the drake.
- The vision that matters is not the pit itself but **the brush and the wall next to it**, because
  that is where the channel starts.

## HOW LILLIA PLAYS THE FIDDLESTICKS MATCHUP

**Who wins the early duel: Lillia, more often than not, and this is one of the very few matchups
where that is true.** He is slow, he has no dash, his drain is interruptible and his clear leaves
him low. Her burn plus her movement speed beats a champion who cannot follow. This is a matchup
she is allowed to be greedy in — before 6, and only before 6.

**Who wins the race: Lillia, comfortably.** Her clear accelerates and his is slow. Ahead on CS
against Fiddlesticks is the expected state, not an achievement — and it is also not the win
condition, because he does not care about the race. The lead has to be spent before his ultimate
is what decides fights.

**When she may contest a crab or an objective:**

- Before he is 6, at almost any even health, in the open.
- After 6, only with vision on the brush and the wall next to the contested ground, or with her
  team there.
- When she has seen him elsewhere **recently and with her own eyes** — remembering that an effigy
  is designed to make exactly this read wrong.

**When she absolutely may not contest:**

- In or next to an enclosed pit, after 6, without vision on the approach. That is his fight and
  she loses it at full health.
- Anywhere she would be fighting while silenced. A Lillia with no abilities has no speed, no burn
  and no exit.
- Off the back of "he was topside a minute ago" — the effigy makes that sentence untrustworthy in
  a way it is not against anyone else in this directory.

**The trap specific to this matchup:** letting the early advantage make her careless late. She
beats him for ten minutes and then walks past one unwarded brush much later and the game is over. The
correct shape of this matchup is **bully him early, and from 6 onward treat every unwarded brush
near an objective as though he is in it** — because being wrong about that once costs more than
every camp the early lead was worth.
