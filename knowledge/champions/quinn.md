# Quinn — the counterpart, and how Lillia plays against her

Read when Quinn is the **enemy jungler**. Quinn is "she"; the player, on Lillia, is "he".

**Start from this:** Quinn in the jungle is an off-meta pick. She is a solo-lane and bottom-lane
marksman-assassin, and a Quinn on the enemy jungle is usually a one-trick or an autofill rather than
a standard draft. That is genuine information — it means her pathing is less likely to follow the
routes he is used to reading, and it means her early clear is probably not the strength of her game.

## What she is

A ranged skirmisher with an enormous roaming speed and a short-range dash that pushes people away
from her. She fights at a distance, she picks off people who are alone, and she crosses the map
faster than any other champion in this directory. What she does not do is win a long fight in a
confined space.

## The kit, as it affects him

**Passive — Harrier.** Valor periodically marks an enemy, and Quinn's **first attack on a marked
target deals bonus physical damage**. It is a large share of her early damage, against champions and
against camps alike. Practically: her damage arrives in spikes tied to the mark, not as steady
output, and a trade that starts on a mark is much worse than one that does not.

**Q — Blinding Assault.** Valor dives the first target hit, damaging the area. At current patch this
**reduces a struck champion's vision radius** rather than blinding their attacks, and it **disarms
the first target if it is a monster**, which is part of how her clear stays safe. This ability has
been reworked before; treat its exact effect as patch-mobile and treat the damage as the reliable
part.

**W — Heightened Senses.** Attacking a marked target gives her attack speed and **movement speed**;
the active **reveals a large area**. She has more scouting in her kit than a normal jungler's ward
budget provides, which means "she cannot know I am here" is a weaker assumption against her.

**E — Vault.** She dashes to an enemy, marks them, and **leaps back off them, knocking them back
slightly and slowing them**. It is a gap-closer that ends with her further away than she started.
Against a champion trying to close distance, it resets the fight to her preferred range — which is
exactly the problem it poses for Lillia.

**R — Behind Enemy Lines.** She channels briefly and gains **very large out-of-combat movement
speed** until she attacks or is interrupted, with a recast that lands for damage and a mark. This is
the defining fact about her on the map: **her position decays as information faster than anyone
else's.** "She was topside twenty seconds ago" is much less reassuring against Quinn than against any
other counterpart in this directory.

I have not written cooldowns, mark timings, damage values, the channel time or the movement-speed
figures.

## Reading her clear

- **Her early clear is respectable rather than fast**, and it leans on the Harrier mark and on the
  disarm keeping her healthy. She is not a champion who takes camps quickly with no items.
- **Expect to be even or ahead on CS against her.** A Lillia running full routes should not be losing
  the farm race to a Quinn jungler, and if he is, the route was interrupted.
- **A Quinn behind on CS has been roaming**, and roaming is what this pick is for — her ultimate makes
  a cross-map gank cheap in a way it is not for anyone else.
- **Her item count matters more than her CS.** She is a marksman-assassin: her damage is item-driven,
  and a Quinn who converted roams into kills is dangerous at a CS number that looks like she is losing.

## Her ganks, and what makes them fail

She arrives from further away than makes sense, at speed, and opens with the mark and the Vault
knockback — or she simply appears in a lane two camps away from where she was last seen.

They fail on distance closing against a target with a dash, and on the fact that her crowd control is
a small knockback and a slow rather than anything that holds someone still. Before her items, a gank
that does not catch someone already low often produces nothing.

## What her items tell you

Attack damage, in one of two shapes.

- **Lethality and burst-shaped items** mean she intends to delete someone caught alone. That is a
  warning about his own solo pathing and about his carries walking the river.
- **Attack speed and critical strike** mean she is playing as a sustained damage carry, which is the
  slower, more scaling version.
- **A completed first item on her** is a real jump — her whole pattern is that the burst on a marked
  target either kills or does not, and items move a lot of targets into the first category.

## What her presence means for drakes and grubs

- **She is not a good contested pit fighter.** She has no sustain, no way to stop a smite, and being
  in an enclosed space with a melee champion is the situation her entire kit exists to avoid.
- What she does instead is **take objectives that nobody is contesting**, and arrive at ones across
  the map faster than the walk suggests.
- Her extra vision from W means a pit she has scouted is a pit she has decided about. She will not
  walk into a fight she cannot see the shape of.

## HOW LILLIA PLAYS THE QUINN MATCHUP

**Who wins the early duel: genuinely close, and it turns on whether he can stay next to her.** She is
ranged with a mark-spike and a reposition; he is melee with damage over time and movement speed that
builds while he keeps hitting things. If he catches her in open ground with Prance already stacked,
her damage does not out-pace his burn. If she kites him with Vault and the slow, he does nothing at
all. **This is a coin flip decided by spacing rather than by health bars, and a coin flip is a
decline.**

**Who wins the race: Lillia, and it is the honest plan here.** His clear accelerates and hers does
not, and a Quinn jungler spends more of her time roaming than clearing. The farm lead is available.

**When he may contest a crab or an objective:**

- In open ground, when he can see her and she does not have room to kite backwards.
- When she is on the other side of the map **and it was very recent** — her ultimate means the usual
  walking-distance arithmetic underestimates her badly.
- When his team is present. She does not want an enclosed fight with numbers against her.

**When he absolutely may not contest:**

- In a long open lane or the river with her at range and space behind her. That is her fight and the
  knockback keeps it that way.
- At low health, anywhere she could arrive at. Her map speed is the thing that makes "she is far
  away" untrue.
- On the strength of a position sighting more than a few seconds old.

**The trap specific to this matchup:** reading the map the normal way. Every other counterpart in this
directory can be tracked by where they were and how fast they walk. Against Quinn, **the map is a
weaker source of truth than usual**, and the Swirlseed rolled down the entrance before crossing is
worth more than a guess about where she went.

## What this file does not claim

I am less confident about Quinn as a jungler than about the other counterparts here, and it is worth
saying so rather than filling the gap.

- **I have not written a clear route or a clear speed for her.** Jungle Quinn is uncommon enough that
  I do not have a sourced route, and a remembered one would be fiction.
- **I have not asserted who wins the level-three duel.** The honest answer depends on her build and on
  spacing in a way I cannot pin down, so the file says "coin flip, decline it" rather than inventing a
  verdict.
- **Her Q's effect has changed across reworks.** What is written above is what the current data files
  say; if a coach finds itself leaning on it, hedge.
- **How common jungle Quinn is at this patch is not something I checked.** Read her presence as
  unusual, but do not tell him she is griefing — a one-trick on an off-meta jungler is often better at
  it than the matchup's reputation suggests.
