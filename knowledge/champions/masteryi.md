# Master Yi — the counterpart, and how Lillia plays against him

Read when Master Yi is the **enemy jungler**.

## What he is

A pure scaling carry with no crowd control at all. He farms, he takes objectives fast, and at some
point he becomes unstoppable unless someone on the enemy team has a button that stops him. He does
not gank well, he does not skirmish well early, and none of that matters if the game goes long and
he was allowed to farm.

**He is a race against a clock, not against a player.** Everything about coaching this matchup is
about that.

## The kit, as it affects her

**Passive — Double Strike.** Every few consecutive attacks he strikes twice. It makes his
auto-attack damage front-load harder than the numbers suggest, and it is part of why his camp clear
is quick with almost no items.

**Q — Alpha Strike.** He **becomes untargetable**, teleports around the target striking several
times, and reappears. Three things follow, all of them practical:

- It deals **bonus damage to monsters**, which is his clear speed.
- **Basic attacks reduce its cooldown**, so in a fight or a clear it comes back far faster than its
  printed cooldown implies.
- The untargetable window **dodges things**, and one of them matters enormously here: **Lilting
  Lullaby will not hit a target who becomes untargetable while it is travelling.** A Yi who presses
  Q as the ult goes out simply does not fall asleep. He can do that on reaction, and good ones do.

**W — Meditate.** A channel that heals him heavily and gives him **large damage reduction while he
channels and briefly afterwards**, with the healing scaling on how much health he is missing. It is
interruptible in the sense that it is a channel, but it is not interruptible by damage. A Yi at low
health who has W available has not lost the duel.

**E — Wuju Style.** Bonus **true damage** on his attacks for a duration. True damage means armour
does not help, which is why he threatens tanks and why "I have more health" is a weaker argument
against Yi than against anyone else in this directory.

**R — Highlander.** Big attack speed, big movement speed, and **immunity to slows**, extended by
every takedown. This is the ability that decides the matchup: Lillia's kite depends on her being
faster than him and on her slow landing, and Highlander answers both at once. Takedowns also refund
a share of his basic ability cooldowns.

I have not written cooldown figures, heal amounts, damage-reduction percentages or Highlander's
duration and extension values.

## Reading his clear

- **Fast, and healthy because of Meditate.** He farms efficiently from the first camp and he does
  not need to back the way a mana champion does.
- **Expect his CS to be high. That is the champion working as intended.** A Yi ahead on CS is not
  news, and coaching "he is out-farming you" as though it were a warning is a misread; the warning
  is his *level and item count*, not the gap.
- **A Yi meaningfully behind on CS is a Yi losing**, because farm is the only thing his early game
  produces. That is the version of him that can be ignored.
- **He has no early gank threat**, so the map is quieter in the first ten minutes of this matchup
  than it looks. The lanes are safer and the jungle is emptier. Both facts are an invitation to
  take his camps.

## His ganks, and what makes them fail

He has no crowd control, so a pre-6 gank requires the lane to already be losing. What he does
instead is **collect**: he arrives after a lane fight has started, and Alpha Strike plus true damage
finishes whoever is already low.

After 6 the gank is Highlander — he runs at the lane immune to slows and faster than anyone can
walk away. It fails to hard crowd control and to nothing else. The honest coaching line is that
"play around his ganks" is not the thing; **denying him the farm and the objectives that get him
there is the thing.**

## What his items tell you

Attack speed and on-hit damage, in every build. The specific items move between patches; what does
not move is what each completion means.

- **His first completed item is a real jump in clear speed and in duel strength.** A Yi one item up
  on her is already past the point where she wins an extended fight.
- **Two completed items is usually where he stops being a jungler and starts being a win condition.**
  That is the threshold worth naming out loud in a game, because it is the moment the team has to
  change what it is doing.
- An early **healing-reduction or anti-heal purchase on his side of the map** is not his — but on
  her team it is the item that makes Meditate stop deciding duels.

## What his presence means for drakes and grubs

- **He takes objectives fast**, with attack speed and the monster bonus on Alpha Strike. A Yi alone
  at a drake takes it quicker than most junglers would with help.
- He is a poor *contested* pit fighter early, because he has no crowd control and no way to stop a
  smite, and a very strong one later, because nothing survives long enough to smite anything.
- **The grub and early drake window is the part of the game she wins.** He is farming; she should be
  spending that time converting into objectives, which is the one currency that does not scale into
  his hands.

## HOW LILLIA PLAYS THE MASTER YI MATCHUP

**Who wins the early duel: Yi, in an extended one, and she cannot make it a short one.** She has no
burst; his Meditate outlasts a damage-over-time champion specifically; his Q dodges her ult and his
true damage ignores her health. There is no honest reading in which Lillia should be duelling Master
Yi at even health at any stage of the game, and this is a matchup where the usual "her fight is the
long one" logic is exactly backwards.

**Who wins the race: nobody, and the race is not the point.** Both clears are fast. Farming evenly
with Master Yi is a losing game because he converts farm into a better late game than she does.
**The win condition is tempo spent on objectives and on his camps, not a CS lead.**

**When she may contest a crab or an objective:**

- Any time before he has his ultimate and a completed item, with her team nearby or with him
  confirmed on the far side of the map.
- When her team has the crowd control present and she is not the one standing in front. She is the
  setup in this fight, never the frontline.
- When the objective can be taken and left. Yi's answer is arriving; the counter to arriving is
  being finished.

**When she absolutely may not contest:**

- Alone, at even health, at any point after he has Highlander. Slow immunity plus his movement speed
  means walking away is not available, and walking away is her entire defensive kit.
- At a drake he is already standing in. He takes it faster than she can contest it and he wins the
  fight that decides the smite.
- Late, ever, without her team. The sleep is one of the few real answers to Highlander — and it is
  an answer her *team* cashes, not one she cashes alone.

**The trap specific to this matchup:** playing the farm game because it feels even. Her CS can match
his all game and she still loses, because he spends his farm on a late game that she does not have.
The correct shape is **early greed**: take his camps, take the early objectives, use the ten quiet
minutes he gives her, and make the game be decided while he is still just a farming champion.
