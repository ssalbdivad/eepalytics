# Zed — the counterpart, and how Lillia plays against him

Read when Zed is the **enemy jungler**. Zed in the jungle is an off-role pick rather than a
standard one, and that is itself worth knowing: he is there because someone wanted to play Zed, and
the jungle is not where his kit is most comfortable.

## What he is

An energy-based assassin who kills one person very quickly from a distance he covered with a
shadow. He has no sustain in his kit, no crowd control worth the name, and an early clear that costs
him real health. He is at his most dangerous against someone standing alone in the jungle — which
describes Lillia for most of the game.

## The kit, as it affects her

**Passive — Contempt for the Weak.** His attacks on low-health targets deal bonus magic damage,
once in a while per target. It is part of why a fight he is losing can still end with her dead: his
damage goes up as her health goes down.

**Q — Razor Shuriken.** A long line of damage, thrown **by him and by every shadow at once**. This
is his clear and his poke, and a double or triple Q from an angle she cannot see the origin of is
most of his early damage.

**W — Living Shadow.** He sends a shadow out, it stands there for a few seconds, and he can swap
places with it. It is his gap-closer, his escape, and the reason he threatens ground he is not
standing on. **A shadow appearing near her is the gank**, not the warning before it.

**E — Shadow Slash.** Area damage from him and his shadows. A shadow's slash **slows**, and hitting
champions with it **reduces the cooldown of Living Shadow** — so a Zed who lands E on her gets his
mobility back sooner.

**R — Death Mark.** He becomes **untargetable**, dashes onto a champion, marks them, and after a
delay the mark detonates for a share of all the damage he dealt in between. It also leaves a shadow
he can swap back to. Two things matter: the untargetable dash means focusing him as he commits is
wasted, and **Lilting Lullaby will not hit a target who becomes untargetable while it is
travelling** — so an ult cast into his R does nothing to him.

I have not written cooldowns, energy costs, shadow durations, the mark delay or the damage-repeat
percentage.

## Reading his clear

- **Slow for a jungler, and it costs him health.** He has no sustain in the kit and energy is a
  tighter leash early than it looks. A Zed who has cleared his own jungle is usually genuinely low.
- **Expect to be ahead of him on CS, and expect the gap to grow.** Against Zed, a Lillia running
  full routes should simply be winning the farm race, and if she is not, the route was interrupted
  rather than the matchup being hard.
- **A Zed behind on CS with kills is the version that wins games.** He converts one lead into a
  snowball better than almost anyone; a Zed even on CS and even on kills is a Zed losing.
- **He wants a squishy target, not a duel.** So his time goes to lanes, not to her jungle — which
  makes counter-farming unusually safe in this matchup and makes her lanes unusually unsafe.

## His ganks, and what makes them fail

He throws a shadow into the lane from behind a wall, swaps, and opens with the double Q. The
approach distance is therefore much longer than his walking range, and a ward on the entrance often
does not see the part that matters.

They fail on **energy** — he cannot both clear and gank without running dry — and on a lane that
respects the shadow the moment it appears. Before 6 his gank is a slow and some damage; it is not
often a kill on its own.

## What his items tell you

Lethality and attack damage, in essentially every build.

- **The first lethality component is a real spike.** His whole pattern is that the burst either
  kills or fails, and each completed piece moves a lot of targets from the second category into the
  first.
- **A completed first item on him** means any of her squishy teammates caught alone is dead, and it
  means she should stop treating a solo path through the river as neutral ground.
- He does not build sustain and he does not build durability early. **A Zed who is low stays low**,
  which is the exploitable half of the matchup.

## What his presence means for drakes and grubs

- He is a poor pit fighter and a poor objective-damage champion: no monster-damage tools, no
  sustain, no way to win a smite contest on raw damage.
- **What he does around objectives is punish the walk to them.** The person who arrives first and
  alone is the person he was waiting for.
- A Zed on the map with his ultimate available makes grouping for an objective more dangerous for
  her carries than for her, which is a fact about where she should be standing, not about whether
  the objective is takeable.

## HOW LILLIA PLAYS THE ZED MATCHUP

**Who wins the early duel: it is closer than either of them should want, and neither should take
it.** He has burst she cannot answer and she has sustained damage he has nothing to heal through.
The honest reading is that at even health before 6 it is close to a coin flip decided by who
started it — and a coin flip is a decline.

**Who wins the race: Lillia, and it should not be close.** His clear is slower and health-expensive
and hers accelerates. **This matchup is won on the farm and objective race**, and a Lillia who is
not ahead of a Zed jungler on CS has interrupted her own route.

**When she may contest a crab or an objective:**

- Nearly always, before he is 6, if she can see him and he is not adjacent. He has no reliable
  crowd control and cannot stop her leaving.
- When he is visibly low. He cannot heal it back, so a hurt Zed is hurt until he backs.
- When her team is present. She wins every fight this champion is not allowed to pick.

**When she absolutely may not contest:**

- Alone, after 6, with his ultimate available and her own health inside his burst. The fight is over
  before her burn has ticked twice.
- Where a shadow can put him on both sides of her — a corridor, a jungle entrance, anywhere with a
  wall he can throw across.
- By casting the ult into his R. The untargetable dash beats the lullaby outright, so the sleep has
  to go out **before** he commits, not as an answer to him committing.

**The trap specific to this matchup:** grading the game on their duel. Zed jungle mostly does not
beat Lillia jungle — it beats her mid laner. Her correct response to a Zed counterpart is to
out-farm him, take the early objectives while he is slowly clearing, and treat every lone walk of a
teammate as the thing she is actually defending. Winning the jungle matchup and losing the game to
it is the specific way this one goes wrong.
