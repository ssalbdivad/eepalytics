# Malphite — the counterpart, and how Lillia plays against him

Read when Malphite is the **enemy jungler**. Malphite is played in the jungle but it is not his
main role, so his presence there is worth noting: he is a slow, safe, tanky clearer whose whole
game is one button at level 6.

## What he is

A tank with a recharging shield, an attack-speed cut that blanks auto-attackers, and an
**unstoppable long-range dash that knocks up everyone it lands on**. Before his ultimate he is a
nuisance who does not die. After it he is the reason a teamfight started on the wrong terms.

## The kit, as it affects her

**Passive — Granite Shield.** He is permanently shielded for a share of his maximum health, and it
**recharges after he has not been hit for a few seconds**. Practically: the first chunk of damage
into a Malphite who has been walking around is absorbed and does not count, so poking him and then
leaving achieves nothing at all. It also recharges between camps, which is why his clear does not
cost him health the way it should.

**Q — Seismic Shard.** A targeted shard that damages, slows, **and gives him the movement speed it
took**. It is his chase, it is how he sticks to her, and it is the reason a Malphite who lands it is
faster than her for the next few seconds. There is no dodging it — it is point-and-click.

**W — Thunderclap.** Passive bonus armour, more while his shield is up, and an active that makes his
next attacks deal extra damage with a small area aftershock. It is his clear and part of why he is
so hard to kill early.

**E — Ground Slam.** Area damage that **reduces attack speed**. Against most junglers this is
devastating; against Lillia it is the least relevant thing in his kit, because her damage comes from
abilities and a burn, not from her attacks. Worth knowing as an advantage she quietly has.

**R — Unstoppable Force.** A long-range dash that cannot be stopped, ending in a **knockup** on
everyone near the landing. It cannot be interrupted, it covers a lot of ground, and it does not miss.
This is the one ability in his kit she has to organise her whole positioning around, because **her
defence is movement and this is the ability that answers movement.**

I have not written cooldowns, shield percentages, slow or attack-speed reduction values, or the
knockup duration.

## Reading his clear

- **Slow-to-average and very safe.** He does not take meaningful damage from camps because the shield
  recharges through them, but he is not fast, and his early ganks are weak without the ultimate.
- **Expect to be ahead of him on CS**, and if she is not, the route was interrupted rather than the
  matchup being hard.
- **His mana is a real early constraint** — every ability costs and none of them are cheap.
- **His level 6 is the only timer that matters on him.** Level, not CS, not items. A Malphite at 5 and
  a Malphite at 6 are different champions and everyone's positioning should change at that line.

## His ganks, and what makes them fail

Before 6 he walks up and slows someone with Q, which is a gank in the same sense that a bad wave is a
gank. It fails to almost anything.

After 6 he does not gank, he **launches**: the ultimate from out of vision, unstoppable, into a
knockup that hands the lane the kill. It fails only when the lane sees him coming far enough out, so
against Malphite the entrance ward is worth more after 6 than before it.

## What his items tell you

This is the champion in this directory where the item feed most changes what he *is*.

- **Armour and health early** means the standard tank: he is unkillable, he is not a damage threat on
  his own, and the ultimate is a setup for someone else. Fighting him is pointless; avoiding the ult
  is the whole job.
- **Ability power in his feed** means the burst build, and it is a different game: the same
  unstoppable engage now kills whoever it lands on. It is not the more common build, but when it
  appears it should change how her carries stand, and it is exactly the kind of thing the item feed
  is for.
- **A completed first item** either way roughly doubles the consequence of his ultimate landing.

## What his presence means for drakes and grubs

- **He is a poor objective-damage champion** and does not take a pit quickly on his own.
- What he does around objectives is start the fight for them — from an angle, unstoppably, on whoever
  is stacked up nearest the pit.
- **The pit is the worst possible place to be standing when he has his ultimate**, because it is
  enclosed, everyone is close together, and the landing knocks up all of it. That is true whichever
  team is taking the objective.

## HOW LILLIA PLAYS THE MALPHITE MATCHUP

**Who wins the early duel: neither, and that is unusual enough to say out loud.** He does not have
the damage to kill her before his items, and she does not have the burst to get through a recharging
shield plus stacking armour. What she does have is the one damage type he is worst against: **her
burn scales on his maximum health**, and he is the highest-maximum-health counterpart in this
directory. Her attack-speed being irrelevant to her also blanks his E. So this is a rare matchup
where an extended fight is not a disaster — it is simply usually pointless, because he can walk away
and so can she.

**Who wins the race: Lillia, clearly.** Her clear accelerates; his is slow and mana-bound. She should
be ahead on CS against a Malphite jungler as a matter of course, and that lead is real because he has
no way to punish her for taking his camps.

**When she may contest a crab or an objective:**

- Before he is 6, more or less freely. He cannot stop her taking it and he cannot kill her for it.
- After 6, when she can see him and there is open ground between them — his ultimate has a long range
  but it is not global, and it is visible while it travels.
- When she has watched him use it. That is a long cooldown and a large window.

**When she absolutely may not contest:**

- Standing in a pit or a corridor with his ultimate available and her team stacked next to her. That
  is the single picture his whole champion is built for.
- At low health inside his Q range, because the slow-and-steal means she does not out-walk him
  afterwards.
- When the item feed shows ability power on him and she is the one at the front. That build turns the
  engage into a kill on her specifically.

**The trap specific to this matchup:** thinking of him as harmless because the jungle matchup is easy.
It is easy — she out-farms him, she out-paces him, she cannot lose a duel to him for a long stretch of
the game — and none of that is what decides the game. **What decides it is whether her team was
standing in a clump when his ultimate came off cooldown**, and the jungler's contribution to that is
the ward on the angle and not being the one who groups first.
