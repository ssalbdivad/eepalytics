# Kayn — the counterpart, and how Lillia plays against him

This file is read when Kayn is the **enemy jungler**. Everything below is about what he is doing
while Lillia clears, and what it means for her.

## What he is

Two champions wearing one name, and which one he becomes is decided in the first ten minutes by
who he has been fighting. Before that he is a mediocre, mobile skirmisher with the best
map-traversal tool in the jungle. After it he is either an unkillable bruiser who heals off every
spell he casts, or an assassin who deletes one person and leaves.

**He is the counterpart who can always leave, and can always arrive.** That single fact should
shape every decision Lillia makes about him.

## The kit, as it affects her

**Passive — The Darkin Scythe.** Damaging enemy champions drops orbs: melee champions drop the
red ones that build toward **Darkin Slayer (Rhaast)**, ranged champions drop blue ones that build
toward **Shadow Assassin**. When the bar fills he unlocks one form, and the other unlocks later.
Two consequences a coach should actually use:

- **He must go back to the fountain to transform**, and the transformation takes several seconds
  during which he is untargetable and cannot act. He comes out of it at **full health and mana,
  with Homeguard**. So a Kayn who has just transformed is not a Kayn who has just backed — he is
  about to appear on the map, fast, whole, and permanently stronger.
- Rhaast heals for a share of the damage his abilities do to champions. Shadow Assassin instead
  gets a burst of bonus magic damage in the first few seconds of each fight with a champion,
  after being out of combat. **One of them wants the long fight and one of them wants the first
  three seconds.** That is the whole matchup, and it flips on the form.

**Q — Reaping Slash.** A short dash and a spin, damage twice. It is his clear, his poke, and his
gap-closer over short distances. As Rhaast it deals a chunk of the target's maximum health, which
is why a Rhaast who is behind is still dangerous to anything with a health bar.

**W — Blade's Reach.** A long swipe that slows. **As Rhaast it knocks up instead** — that is the
hard CC in the matchup and it only exists after he has taken the red form. As Shadow Assassin it
is longer-ranged and he can move while casting.

**E — Shadow Step.** Move speed, ghosting, and **he walks through terrain** for several seconds,
healing when he first enters a wall. This is the ability that makes him different from everyone
else in this directory. There is no wall between him and anywhere. A jungle entrance is not a
chokepoint against Kayn, a ward on an entrance covers less than it does against anyone else, and
"he is on the other side of that wall" means he is roughly one second away. Being immobilised or
staying out of terrain too long ends it early — which is the only way anyone ever catches him.

**R — Umbral Trespass.** He infests a champion he has damaged recently and is **untargetable**
while inside, then bursts out for heavy damage. Rhaast's version does max-health damage and heals
him. It is an execute and an invulnerability window at once: focusing Kayn as he ults is throwing
the damage away.

I have not written his cooldowns, orb counts, transformation timing thresholds or healing
percentages. Those move with patches and none of them is worth a wrong number.

## Reading his clear

- **Pre-transform Kayn is a farming champion.** His early clear is respectable and his early
  ganks are not, so a Kayn even or slightly ahead on CS before his form is playing normally.
- **A Kayn meaningfully behind on CS early has been chasing orbs**, not camps — he has been
  poking champions in lanes to fill the bar faster. That Kayn transforms sooner and has less
  gold. He is more likely to be near a lane, not near a camp.
- **The form is the real timer, not the clock.** The question "has he transformed yet" matters
  more than his CS, his level or his items, because his duel changes completely at that moment.
- **A Kayn who just recalled and did not appear again for several seconds was transforming.**
  Expect him back on the map immediately, at full health, moving fast.

## His ganks, and what makes them fail

He ganks through walls. Not across the river, not down the lane — **through the wall behind the
lane**, which means the usual gank-warding intuition is wrong against him. His entrance is not
covered by the entrance ward.

They fail for two reasons. Before the form he has **no hard CC** — W only slows, so a gank
against anyone with a dash or a summoner spell is a slow and a wave. And his E ends early if he
is immobilised, so a lane that lands anything on him mid-approach takes the approach away.

Once Rhaast exists, the W knockup makes those same ganks lethal, and after 6 a Shadow Assassin
gank is E in, R on someone he already touched, and the target is dead before the screen catches up.

## What his items tell you

Item metas move between patches, so read the **class** of what appears rather than the name.

- **Lethality and cooldown-shaped assassin items** mean Shadow Assassin: burst, one target, no
  interest in a long fight. Against that Kayn, the danger is being alone; the safety is being
  with anyone.
- **Bruiser items with health and sustain** mean Rhaast: he wants the extended fight, he heals
  through it, and every second she stays in it is worse for her.
- **A completed first item on him with none on her** is the moment to stop contesting anything
  in melee range, whichever form he took.

## What his presence means for drakes and grubs

- His E means **he is at the pit sooner than his position on the map suggests**. Reading "he is
  topside" and walking to a drake is an assumption about walking distance that does not hold
  against this champion.
- Rhaast is a genuine threat in the pit — max-health damage, healing, a knockup. Shadow Assassin
  is not a pit fighter, he is a punisher of whoever walks to the pit alone.
- The smite fight against either form is a bad fight for Lillia **on her own**. With her team
  there and her ult held for the moment they commit, it is the fight she is built for.

## HOW LILLIA PLAYS THE KAYN MATCHUP

This is the section that matters.

**Who wins the early duel: before his form, it is close and neither of them should want it.**
Lillia has no burst, Kayn has no CC, and the fight ends in someone walking away — except he
walks away better, because E is a real escape and Prance is only an escape if it was already
stacked. A duel where he can leave and she cannot is not an even duel even when the health bars
say it is.

**After the form, she loses it, and how she loses it depends on which one he took.** Rhaast
out-sustains her in exactly the fight she is built for — that is the uncomfortable one, because
the long kiting fight is her plan and it is also his. Shadow Assassin kills her in the first
seconds, before the burn has done anything at all.

**Who wins the race: Lillia, and this is where she should be playing.** Kayn's clear does not
accelerate, and he spends time on champions to fill the orb bar that she spends on camps. If he
is off poking a lane, that is a route she gets to run uninterrupted.

**When she may contest a crab or an objective:**

- **Before he has transformed**, at even or better health, in open ground, with her ult available
  and something already touched by dust.
- When she can name where he is **and it is not adjacent to a wall near her**, which is a stricter
  condition against Kayn than against anyone else.
- When she is meaningfully ahead in level, with the Swirlseed rolled through the entrance first.

**When she absolutely may not contest:**

- Against Rhaast, at even health, ever. His fight is the long one and he heals through it.
- Against Shadow Assassin when she is alone and he has his ult, at any health that his burst
  covers.
- Immediately after he has been missing for several seconds near his own fountain — that is a
  transformation, and he is arriving at full health with Homeguard.
- At low mana. She cannot fight him and she cannot outrun a ghosted champion walking through the
  wall she is trying to put between them.

**The trap specific to this matchup:** treating a wall as protection. Every jungler in this
directory except Kayn is slowed down by terrain. Backing off "behind the wall", clearing the camp
"on the safe side", pathing home "the long way round" — all of that is a plan built against a
champion who has to walk around, and he does not. The honest version of the rule is that against
Kayn, distance is the only safety, and the Swirlseed is the only cheap way to buy information
about which side of the wall he is on.
