# Lillia — how she actually works

Written for a coach who has one-tricked her. Everything here is champion identity: the
mechanics below have not changed in any patch she has existed through. Where a figure moves
between patches it is marked; where I am not sure of a number I have left it out and said so
rather than guessing, because a wrong number sounds exactly like a right one.

## The kit, in the order it matters

**Passive — Dream-Laden Bough (Dream Dust).** Every ability she lands coats the target in Dream
Dust, a burn that ticks for a few seconds and scales with the target's **maximum health**. Two
consequences run through her entire game: she does percentage damage, so she hurts tanks far more
than her raw AP suggests, and **the dust is the prerequisite for her ultimate**. Nothing else in
the kit cares who is a squishy.

**Q — Blooming Blows.** A ring of damage around her, and every enemy hit grants a stack of
**Prance**, which is movement speed. The stacks build as she keeps hitting things and **decay a
few seconds after she stops**. This is the single most important sentence about Lillia: her
movement speed is a resource she earns by continuing to fight or clear, and loses by standing
around. It is also why Q is a ring rather than a cone — she is supposed to be *moving through*
the thing she is damaging, not standing in front of it.

**W — Watch Out! Eep!** A short wind-up, then a strike in a circle in front of her, with a small
forward hop built into the wind-up. Two things matter. It deals **bonus damage to monsters**,
which makes it the engine of the clear, and its **centre hits much harder than its edge**, which
makes camp placement a real skill: a W that catches the big monster in the middle of the circle
is worth roughly a whole extra rotation of Q. The hop is a *step*, not a dash — it will not cross
terrain and it does not get her out of anything. Treating W as an escape is how she dies.

**E — Swirlseed.** She rolls a seed in a line. It travels a long way, rolls **over terrain**, and
**grants vision of what it rolls past and reveals the first champion it hits**. The longer it
rolls uninterrupted before it lands, the harder it hits and the harder it slows. So E is three
tools wearing one coat: a scouting ward she can throw over a wall, a long-range slow that sets up
a gank, and the cheapest way to apply dust to someone standing outside her range. A Lillia who
only ever uses E as damage in a fight is throwing away the best information tool in her kit.

**R — Lilting Lullaby.** Global. Every enemy **who currently has Dream Dust on them** becomes
drowsy, then falls asleep a moment later; waking them with damage deals bonus damage. Enemies
without dust are not affected at all. **So the ult is a finisher on a fight she has already
touched, never an opener.** The order is: land Q or E or W on them first, *then* ult. A Lillia
who presses R at a fresh, untouched enemy team achieves literally nothing and has spent a
two-minute cooldown to do it. Its other honest use is defensive — dust one diver, sleep them,
walk away — and as a global it can rescue a fight on the other side of the map that she is
nowhere near, which is the one thing about her that reads on a scoreboard as "she was useless
that fight" when she was not.

## Mana, which decides her route

She is mana-hungry in a way that shapes the whole early game. Q is spammed, W is expensive, and
a full first clear leaves her close to empty. Practical consequences, and these are the ones a
live coach should actually say:

- **A Lillia at low mana cannot clear and cannot gank.** She has no third option. If mana is low
  and blue is dead, the honest advice is to back — not to path toward a lane.
- **She wants blue buff, both of them, more than most junglers want anything.** A route that
  gives blue away for a fast start pays for it two camps later.
- Running out mid-camp is worse than backing one camp early, because the camp then takes twice as
  long with autos and she loses the Prance stacks she was carrying into the next one.
- A back that comes with a full mana bar and a completed component is not lost tempo. A back at
  40% HP and no gold is.

## The clear, and why it accelerates

The standard full clear at recent patches is **Blue → Gromp → Wolves → Raptors → Red → Scuttle**,
with the leashless mirror (**Raptors → Krugs → Red → Wolves → Gromp → Blue**) as the common
alternative when she wants to end up on the other side of the map. Neither is sacred; what is
sacred is *finishing the one she started*.

Her clear gets **faster as it chains** because Prance is still stacked when she arrives at the
next camp. The back half of a route is meaningfully quicker than the front half. That produces
the defining Lillia mistake and its fix:

- **An interrupted route costs her more than it costs any other jungler**, because she pays the
  ramp-up cost twice and walks the gap at base movement speed. Hovering a lane for fifteen
  seconds and returning to the camp is not a fifteen-second loss, it is a fifteen-second loss
  plus the stacks.
- The correct clear **kites the camp toward the next camp** while killing it. She should finish
  a camp already halfway to the following one, not standing where she started.
- So a low CS-per-minute on Lillia is almost always **an interrupted route, not a slow one**.
  Coaching "clear faster" at her is useless; coaching "finish the route you started" is the
  actual fix.

## What she wins and what she loses

**She loses the early even duel to nearly everything.** No burst, no reliable hard CC before 6,
damage that arrives over time. Level 2 and level 3 skirmishes are coin flips she should decline,
and playing "passively" early on Lillia is usually correct play rather than timidity.

**She wins the long fight with room to move.** Percentage-health burn, a ring she wants to
rotate through, and movement speed that grows the longer she is swinging. Every fight she wins
looks the same: open ground, no wall behind her, circling, dust on three people, ult when they
commit. Every fight she loses looks the same too: a corridor, a pit, a tri-brush, or a
tower-dive, where movement speed does nothing and she gets hit once and dies.

**Level 6 is the real spike**, and 6 with a dust-first habit is a different champion from 6
without it. Getting there on time beats almost any individual early skirmish.

## Items

Item names and exact stats move between patches — treat the following as the shape of her build,
not as a shopping list to recite.

- **Liandry's** first, on essentially every patch she has existed through, because her entire
  damage profile is a max-health burn and the item multiplies exactly that. It is also why she
  scales into tanks.
- **Zhonya's** is a real power spike on her specifically, not a panic item: her ult wants her to
  live through the moment right after she casts it, and a sleeping enemy team plus a stasis is a
  fight-winning sequence.
- **Riftmaker / Cosmic Drive / Rylai's** occupy the second and third slots depending on patch.
  Cosmic Drive and Rylai's both fit her identity for the same reason — one adds to the movement
  speed she already stacks, the other makes the kiting circle land.
- Boots choice on her has genuinely swung between Sorcerer's and Swiftness across patches. I am
  not confident which is correct on today's patch, so I will not assert one.

What matters live is not the name of the item but the fact that **her first completed item is
when she stops being farm-only**. Before it she is racing; after it she can take a fight she
picked.

## Drakes and objectives

- Her W's bonus monster damage means she takes objectives at a respectable rate, but she is
  **poor in the 1v1 pit fight** — no burst to close it, no way out if it goes wrong.
- Her real drake control is **setup**: arrive early, have the camps on that side already taken,
  have the dust on someone, and be at full mana. A drake she walks to at 30% mana she is not
  actually contesting.
- Her best objective fight is **not** the smite duel; it is the ult on an enemy team stacked in
  the pit. That is the highest-value R in the game and it is worth holding the ult for.
- E over the wall into the pit before committing is free information about whether they are
  already there.

## Invading

- **She may invade when the counterpart is confirmed elsewhere and she is taking a camp, not a
  fight.** Her fast clear means a stolen camp is genuinely stolen — she is in and out before the
  route cost matters.
- **She absolutely may not invade to duel**, at any point before her first item, and rarely
  after: she loses even fights and she has no escape once she is in a corridor.
- **She may not invade at low mana**, ever. She cannot clear the camp she came for and she
  cannot fight for it.
- If she cannot say where the enemy jungler is, the honest answer is not "ward it" — she has no
  wards in hand most of the time — it is **roll the Swirlseed down the entrance first**, which is
  a thing she can actually do in the next three seconds.

## The mistakes she makes, by stage

**Levels 1–3.** W thrown off-centre so the big monster catches the edge. W used on a small camp
where the cooldown is worth more than the damage. Standing still to clear, so Prance never
stacks. Starting a route she is going to abandon at the first ping.

**Levels 4–6.** Running out of mana mid-route and then walking to a lane instead of backing.
Hovering a losing lane for twenty seconds and arriving with no stacks, no mana and no ult.
Contesting a crab against a jungler who beats her at even HP.

**Post-6.** The wasted ult — pressed at an untouched enemy, or pressed as an engage. Using R to
run away from a fight her team is winning. Using E to chip someone rather than to check the wall
she is about to walk past.

**Late.** Standing still to output damage instead of orbiting the fight, which turns her
strongest trait off. Getting caught in a corridor or a jungle entrance where her speed is
worthless. Being the one who walks in first, on a champion whose whole design is arriving after
the fight has started.

## Where she dies

The only reposition in the kit is the W hop, and it is a step. Everything else about leaving is
Prance, which means **the escape has to already be stacked before she needs it**. That gives one
genuinely champion-specific read on her deaths: a death in a corridor, a pit, or a tri-brush is
not a positioning mistake made at the moment of death, it is a mistake made ten seconds earlier
when she entered a place her speed could not get her out of.

## Deliberate omissions

I have not written down: a clear *time* to level 4 (the sources this repo uses do not publish
one, and a remembered one would be fiction), exact Prance stack counts or movement-speed values,
Dream Dust duration in seconds, drowsy and sleep durations, or ability cooldowns. All of those
move with patches and none of them are worth a wrong number.

## Live coaching

Half-sentences, said out loud after a number the client has already proved. **No digits** —
a figure here would be a claim about the game, and every figure he hears must come from the
client. Anything with a number in it is refused at load time, deliberately.

Several clauses per key is intentional: a fixed clause per kind means the identical half-sentence
every time that kind fires, and sameness is what made him stop listening. Each one has to end on
something he can DO in the next thirty seconds.
