# Xin Zhao — the counterpart, and how Lillia plays against him

This file exists to be read when Xin Zhao is the **enemy jungler**. Everything below is about
what he is doing while Lillia is clearing, and what it means for her.

## What he is

A diver. Melee, mana, 175 attack range, and a kit whose every piece exists to get him onto one
person and keep him there. He has a targeted dash, a knock-up, a slow, a heal that comes from
attacking, and an ultimate that pushes everyone else out of the fight and then makes him
immune to them. He is not a burst assassin and he is not a scaling carry — he is the champion
who arrives, picks someone, and stays until one of them is dead.

He is also an **early-game** jungler. Everything that makes him frightening is online at level
3 and nothing about his kit compounds the way Lillia's does. The clock is his opponent.

## The kit, as it affects her

**Passive — Determination.** His basic attacks, and the strikes of his W, build stacks; the
third one consumes them, deals bonus physical damage and **heals him for a percentage of his
maximum health**. Two consequences. First, **he does not leave camps low** — like Briar, his
health bar is restored by the act of clearing, so "he just did his whole topside, he must be
hurt" is false about Xin Zhao. Second, in a fight the heal is on a three-attack cycle, so a
duel that goes past a few seconds is a duel he is being paid for.

**Q — Three Talon Strike.** His next three attacks hit harder, **each one takes a second off
his other cooldowns**, and the third **knocks the target up**. The cooldown reduction is the
underrated half: a Xin Zhao who is landing attacks has his dash and his W back sooner than the
tooltip suggests, which is why "his E must still be down" is a bad assumption during a fight.

**W — Wind Becomes Lightning.** An arc of slashes, then a long thrust — a thousand units — that
slows hard and **reveals and marks the farthest champion or large monster it hits**. This is a
real reveal, not a ward: a Xin Zhao poking a spear down a river corridor learns something. It
is also his only long-range tool, so it is usually the first thing that touches her.

**E — Audacious Charge.** A dash **to a target**, with an area slow and a burst of attack speed
on arrival. Its range extends against a target he has Challenged with W or with his ultimate
learned. The word that matters is *target*: it needs a unit. He can leap off a minion, a jungle
monster or a pet as easily as off a champion, so terrain and distance are far weaker protection
against him than they look, and a wave standing between them is a bridge rather than a wall.

**R — Crescent Guard.** A sweep that deals damage based on the target's **current** health,
**knocks back and stuns everyone he has not Challenged**, and then, for a few seconds, makes him
**invulnerable to champions outside the circle**. Read that twice, because both halves matter to
Lillia. The knock-back is how he removes four people from a fight and keeps the fifth — and the
one he keeps is the one his W or his attacks marked. The invulnerability means that once his
ultimate is up, damage from outside the circle does nothing at all; standing at range and
throwing things at him is, briefly, literally worthless.

I have not written his cooldowns at each rank, his heal percentages, his damage numbers or the
exact knock-back distance — those move with patches and none of them are worth a wrong number.

## Reading his clear

- **He clears fast and finishes healthy.** Expect his CS to keep pace with a good Lillia route,
  and read a level CS count as normal rather than as a sign she is behind.
- **If he is meaningfully ahead in level**, he has been full-clearing, which is the safest time
  for her to take a far camp or a crab — and the worst time to path toward a lane hoping to
  catch him out of position, because he is not out of position, he is on a route.
- **If he is behind in level from about 5 onward**, he has been near lanes. A Xin Zhao who has
  traded farm for presence is the more dangerous version, not the less, and the next place he
  appears is a lane.
- **Watch for his first completed fighting item while she has none.** His whole kit converts a
  raw stat advantage straight into a won duel, and there is no phase of the game where he is
  better at fighting than the moment he finishes an item first.
- **Boots and a heavy defensive item early** say he intends to arrive and stay; that is the
  build that punishes a contested pit hardest.

## His ganks, and what makes them fail

He ganks with E — one dash onto a target — and converts with Q's knock-up. That is the whole
pattern, and it is why he wants the lane to have a minion, a pet or a ward-less flank to dash
off. They fail for two reasons: **he is seen walking in**, because he has no stealth and nothing
that hides an approach, and **he arrives with no follow-up** — a Xin Zhao who has spent his
knock-up and has his target walk away has nothing left but attack speed.

Once he has committed, **he has no exit**. His dash is an entrance, not an escape; there is no
second one, and his ultimate keeps him in the fight rather than out of it. A Xin Zhao dive that
does not kill is usually a dead Xin Zhao, which is the shape of nearly every fight he loses.

## HOW LILLIA PLAYS THE XIN ZHAO MATCHUP

This is the section that matters.

**Who wins the early duel: Xin Zhao, decisively.** He out-sustains her through the passive,
out-damages her at every point before her first completed item, and holds a knock-up she cannot
avoid once his dash has landed. She must not take an even-health 1v1 with him before her first
item. Not at a crab, not to defend a camp, not because he looks low — he will not be low. The
camp is worth less than the death.

**Who wins the race: Lillia, and this is the whole plan.** Both clears are fast; his is fast
and healthy, hers is fast and **getting faster** as the Prance stacks build. The difference is
that his speed does not carry between camps and hers does, and his power stops compounding
around the time hers starts. So the posture is **trade camps, never trade blows.** If he is in
her raptors, the answer is to be in his, not to walk back and contest.

**The one mechanic to plan around: he needs a target to dash to.** Everything about staying
alive in this matchup is about not being the nearest dashable thing when he arrives. That means
fighting in open ground rather than a corridor, not standing in her own wave when he is unlocated,
and starting to leave *before* he is in E range rather than after — her speed is an exit and it
only works if it is already stacked.

**When she may contest a crab or a pit:**

- When she knows where he is and it is not here. A Swirlseed rolled down the river or into the
  entrance answers that in two seconds, and it is the cheapest information in her kit.
- When she is meaningfully ahead in level or health, **with open ground behind her** and a way
  out that is not through him.
- When his ultimate is not a factor — because once it is up, a contested pit is a fight where
  her team's damage from outside the circle stops existing for four seconds.

**When she absolutely may not:**

- At even health before her first item. Ever.
- At low mana: she cannot fight him and she cannot leave.
- In a pit or a corridor, where his knock-up and his knock-back have a wall to work against and
  her speed has nowhere to take her.
- When she cannot say where he is. Against a champion whose engage is one targeted dash, "he is
  probably topside" is not information.

**The trap specific to this matchup:** punishing him after a clear. He heals through his clear,
so the health bar she is imagining is not the one he has. The moment to punish Xin Zhao is
**after a failed dive, or once his ultimate has been spent**, not after a camp.
