# Jarvan IV — the counterpart, and how Lillia plays against him

Read when Jarvan IV is the **enemy jungler**.

## What he is

A fast, healthy-clearing bruiser with a two-button knockup that also covers a long distance, a
shield, armour shred, and **an ultimate that builds walls around the person he lands on**. He is one
of the most reliable early junglers in the game and his ultimate is, specifically and literally, the
removal of Lillia's escape.

## The kit, as it affects her

**Passive — Martial Cadence.** His first attack on a target deals bonus damage based on their current
health. It front-loads his trades and it is why his first hit in any skirmish is bigger than his
items suggest.

**Q — Dragon Strike.** He extends his lance in a line, damaging and **removing a share of the
target's armour**. On its own it is minor. Its real function is the second half:

**E — Demacian Standard.** He plants a flag that damages and gives attack speed to allies near it.
**If Q connects with the flag, he pulls himself to it and knocks up everyone in the path.** That
combination — flag then lance — is his gap-closer, his crowd control and his gank, all in one
sequence, and it covers a surprising distance. It is also his escape, because the flag can be thrown
away from the fight and over walls.

**W — Golden Aegis.** A shield that grows with the number of enemy champions nearby, and a slow on
everyone near him. The slow is short and it is enough. Against a champion whose defence is movement
speed, the shield and slow together mean the trade goes worse than the health bars predicted.

**R — Cataclysm.** He **leaps to a champion** and creates **impassable terrain in a ring around
them** for a few seconds, and he can collapse it early. This is the ability that decides the matchup.
Lillia's escape is speed; speed inside a circle of walls is nothing. There is no version of this ult
landing on her where her movement plan still works.

I have not written cooldowns, shred percentages, shield values, knockup duration or the wall's
duration.

## Reading his clear

- **Fast and healthy.** The flag's attack speed plus his shield means his camps go down quickly and
  cost him little, and he is on the map early and often.
- **Expect his CS to keep pace.** Even CS against Jarvan is normal; being meaningfully ahead of him
  means she ran uninterrupted routes while he was ganking.
- **A Jarvan behind on CS has been at lanes, and his ganks convert**, because a knockup on a flag pull
  is not something a lane dodges by walking. Check the lanes before assuming he fell behind.
- **He is strongest in the first fifteen minutes.** A Jarvan who did not get ahead early is a bruiser
  with one good engage; a Jarvan who did is running the map.

## His ganks, and what makes them fail

Flag over the wall, lance into it, knockup, shield, and the lane is dead. The approach can start from
outside the lane entirely, which means the usual entrance ward covers less than it should.

They fail when the **flag is seen and the lane moves before the lance connects** — there is a real
window between the two casts — and when the target has a dash or a summoner spell. They do not fail
because of mana; his costs are modest.

## What his items tell you

Bruiser items — attack damage with health and resistances — in essentially every build.

- **His first completed item** is the point where the knockup sequence stops being a setup and starts
  being most of a kill. For her, that is the line at which even-health contests near him end.
- **Heavy durability early** means he plans to be the one starting fights around objectives. The
  ultimate is a tool for locking someone down for his team, and durability is what lets him use it on
  a front line rather than as an assassination.
- He builds no mobility beyond the flag. Nothing in his feed will make his engage longer-ranged than
  it already is, which is the only predictable thing about him.

## What his presence means for drakes and grubs

- **He is a strong pit fighter**: armour shred on the objective, a shield, and a knockup that wins a
  smite contest by removing the other jungler from the fight for a moment.
- **His ultimate in a pit is at its worst for her.** The pit is already enclosed; his ult adds a
  second ring of walls inside it. Being ulted at a drake is being told the fight is now stationary,
  which is the fight she loses.
- The early objectives — grubs especially — are ones he is favoured to contest at even numbers, and
  she should be reading them as his unless her team is present.

## HOW LILLIA PLAYS THE JARVAN MATCHUP

**Who wins the early duel: Jarvan, clearly.** Front-loaded passive damage, a knockup, a shield, armour
shred, and a slow. She has no burst to punish the window after his combo and no crowd control before
6. At even health in the first ten minutes this is not a fight she takes, and the flag on the ground
near her is the warning that she is about to be in one.

**Who wins the race: about even, with an edge to her if her routes stay whole.** Both clears are fast.
His time goes to lanes more than hers does, which is the gap she can open — but only by running full
routes rather than by responding to the lane he just ganked.

**When she may contest a crab or an objective:**

- In open ground, when she has seen his ultimate used, and with Prance already stacked.
- When he has just committed the flag-and-lance somewhere else. Both halves of his engage are on one
  cooldown chain and she can watch it being spent.
- When she is arriving second, to a fight her team has already started, with dust on him and the sleep
  ready for the moment he leaps.

**When she absolutely may not contest:**

- Inside a pit, at even health, with his ultimate available. The walls plus the pit make her kit
  irrelevant.
- Next to a freshly planted flag. That is not decoration; it is the first half of a knockup that is
  already in flight.
- At low health anywhere he can reach, because his engage covers far more ground than his walking
  speed suggests and the ultimate is a leap, not a walk.

**The trap specific to this matchup:** treating his ultimate as damage. It is not damage, it is
**terrain** — and terrain is the one thing that turns her off entirely. Every other counterpart in
this directory can be escaped by being faster than them. He cannot be, because he does not have to
catch her: he builds a room around her and waits. The habit that answers it is fighting near open
ground and spending the sleep on the moment he leaps rather than on the fight afterwards.
