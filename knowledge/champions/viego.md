# Viego — the counterpart, and how Lillia plays against him

Read when Viego is the **enemy jungler**.

## What he is

A resourceless skirmisher who gets a second life out of every corpse on the battlefield. He is a
strong early duelist, a fast clearer and a champion whose whole design is that **a fight he was
losing becomes a fight he wins the moment anyone dies near him** — including a dead teammate of
his own.

## The kit, as it affects her

**Passive — Sovereign's Domination.** Every enemy who dies near him leaves a wraith. Attacking the
wraith **possesses** the corpse: he heals for a share of that champion's maximum health, gets
their basic abilities and their items for a short time, and keeps a free cast of his own ultimate.
The practical translations:

- **A kill near Viego is not the end of the fight, it is a refill.** Chasing him low after he has
  just killed someone is walking into a full-health champion wearing someone else's kit.
- The possession has a duration and ends on its own, and it ends early if he takes too much damage
  during it. Running the clock down is a real answer where killing him is not.
- He plays the whole game looking for the first kill in a skirmish, because the first kill hands
  him the second.

**Q — Blade of the Ruined King.** The passive is the important half: his attacks do bonus damage
based on the target's **current** health, and his first attack on someone he has recently hit with
an ability strikes twice and heals him. This is why his duel ramps so fast and why his clear is
quick and healthy without items.

**W — Spectral Maw.** A charged dash that **stuns** the first enemy hit, longer the longer he
charged. It is his engage and the only hard CC in the kit. He is slowed while charging it, which
is the tell.

**E — Harrowed Path.** He sends a spectre at a wall and surrounds it in mist. Inside the mist he is
**camouflaged** and moves and attacks faster. He approaches along walls, not through open ground,
and the mist is visible on the ground — **seeing mist on a wall near a lane is seeing the gank
before it happens.**

**R — Heartbreaker.** He teleports a short distance and hits **the champion with the lowest
percentage of health remaining**, with damage scaling on their missing health, knocking others
back. It is an execute in a kit that already resets off executes.

I have not written his cooldowns, possession duration, healing percentages or stun timings.

## Reading his clear

- **Fast and healthy.** His Q passive sustains him through camps with no mana cost at all, so he
  is never held back by a resource bar and never has to back for mana.
- **Expect his CS to keep pace with a good Lillia clear.** Level CS against Viego is normal, not a
  warning sign.
- **A Viego meaningfully behind on CS has been fighting**, and a Viego who has been fighting has
  probably been winning some of them, because a Viego who loses fights early does not keep taking
  them. Behind on CS and even or ahead on kills is the dangerous version.
- **He has no mana, so "he must be low on resources" is never true of him.** The only thing that
  slows him down is his own health bar, and his Q passive refills that off camps.

## His ganks, and what makes them fail

He arrives along a wall inside his own mist, camouflaged, and opens with the charged W stun. The
mist on the ground is the warning and it is a real one: it is placed before he commits.

They fail when the stun misses — it is a slow, telegraphed charge — and when the lane simply sees
the mist. They do not fail because of his mana, his health or his cooldowns, because he has no
mana, sustains his own health, and only needs the one button.

## What his items tell you

He is an attack-damage skirmisher; the tell is what kind.

- **Attack speed and on-hit items** point at the extended duel and at objective damage. That Viego
  is faster at a pit and harder to out-last.
- **Crit and lethality-shaped items** point at the burst-and-reset pattern: he wants one squishy
  dead, and then he wants to be that squishy.
- **A completed first item on him** is where his duel stops being merely strong and starts being
  unlosable for a champion with no burst, which describes her exactly.

## What his presence means for drakes and grubs

- He is a good pit fighter: sustain through the objective, a stun for the smite contest, and an
  execute that the low health bar of an objective fight hands him for free.
- The bigger danger is structural: **he wins objective fights by winning the fight before the
  objective**, because one death on her team gives him a full-health second champion inside the
  pit.
- The one honest consolation: he has no way to steal an objective from range. Everything he does
  at a pit requires him to be standing in it.

## HOW LILLIA PLAYS THE VIEGO MATCHUP

**Who wins the early duel: Viego, clearly.** Current-health damage, a double-hit heal, a stun and
no resource bar against a champion with no burst and no hard CC before 6. She does not take an
even-health duel with Viego at any point in the early game, and the deeper reason is worse than the
damage: even when she is winning it, anything that dies nearby hands it back to him.

**Who wins the race: nobody, and that is the useful part.** Both clears are fast and neither
jungler is starved. Since the race is a draw, the matchup is decided by **who takes fewer fights
they did not choose** — and every fight he chooses, he wins. So the plan is not to out-farm him,
it is to keep taking objectives while declining the skirmishes, which is the one thing her speed
is actually for.

**When she may contest a crab or an objective:**

- When she knows where he is and it is not here, and she can take the thing and leave before he
  crosses. A stolen camp is a win against Viego in a way a duel never is.
- When her team is present in numbers, with her ult held for the moment he commits — a drowsy Viego
  mid-charge is one of the few clean ways this matchup goes her way.
- When he has recently used his W and she watched it happen. Without the stun, his gank and his
  duel both get meaningfully worse.

**When she absolutely may not contest:**

- At even health, alone, at any point. This is the flat rule of the matchup.
- Anywhere with mist on the wall next to her. That is not ambient, it was placed.
- Immediately after anyone has died nearby — hers or his. For the next few seconds he is a
  different champion at full health.
- In a corridor or a pit where the W stun lands and her speed does nothing.

**The trap specific to this matchup:** finishing a fight. She burns him down, he is visibly low,
and one more rotation kills him — except somebody else on the floor makes him whole. Against Viego
the correct instinct after a won skirmish is to **leave and take something**, not to chase the kill.
